# Greenhollow

A Roman village simulation played in the browser: villagers with needs, jobs, families, gossip,
crime and a town watch, drawn on a canvas. The owner is hands-off with GitHub: do the git work
(branches, commits, pushes, merging to main when tests pass) yourself.

## Commands
- `npm run build`: joins `src/` into `dist/index.html`, the single self-contained file that is played and published.
- `npm run typecheck`: TypeScript check (`tsc --noEmit`). Must stay at zero errors.
- `npm test`: build, typecheck, then the headless-browser tests in `tests/run.mjs` (about 2 minutes).
  `node tests/run.mjs food` runs only tests whose name contains "food".
- After changing `src/`, run `npm run build` and commit `dist/index.html` with the change (CI checks it is up to date).

## How the code is put together (read before editing)
- `src/*/*.ts` are **classic scripts sharing one global scope**, not ES modules. `scripts/build.mjs`
  concatenates them in the order of `src/order.json`, strips types with esbuild and inlines them with
  `src/styles.css` into `src/index.html`. A new file must be added to `src/order.json` (the build fails otherwise).
- Order matters: later files patch earlier ones, e.g. `life2/glue.ts` wraps `lifeHud`
  (`const _hud = lifeHud; lifeHud = function ...`). Those spots carry `@ts-expect-error -- deliberate patch`.
- Function declarations are hoisted across the whole joined script, so if a name is declared twice the
  **last** one wins everywhere. Don't declare a function twice; replace it in place.
- `art/painters.ts` is a sealed IIFE (`const ART = ...`) holding all sprite and tile painting; the game reaches it only through `ART.*`.
- Folders: `world/` generation and island, `life/` and `life2/` village life (life2 = the Roman layer: events,
  gossip, trades, law, daily life, grudges), `ai/` villager plans and decisions, `social/` and `dialogue/`
  conversations, `ceremonies/`, `player/` controlling a villager, `render/` drawing, `ui/` panels, input and the main loop.
- Types are loose for now (`strict: false`, many `: any` from the JS conversion). Tighten gradually: prefer
  adding real interfaces (Person, Town, Game) over more `any`.

## Controls (src/player/items.ts, src/life2/controls.ts)
**Auto controls** (`IT_AUTO = true` in `player/items.ts`; set it false to get the old hand modes and belt tools back): in the world a bump does the obvious thing: talk to a villager (or buy from a seller), search a crate or barrel (once a day), fell a tree (axe), break a rock (hammer), fish (rod), work a plot (hoe, and the seed you have most of, or the one in hand).
The tool is picked for you (`itFits` checks you own it; `itWearTool` wears it; the tool strip beside the hotbar shows wear and lights the one about to be used). The hotbar has **ten** slots (1-0) and holds only seeds and lockpicks (`n.belt`, drag them in from the inventory **I**; pressing the held slot puts it away; lockpicks must be held to pick a lock). Q does nothing; Shift and the old keys do nothing.
**Field rule:** a plot that needs work is worked only when you start from rest beside it (`p.fresh`, set by a press after a quarter-second of rest, cleared by stepping onto anything that is not a crop tile), so walking over a field on your way somewhere never ploughs it, and working a row keeps going as you hold the key. Pushing, smashing and turning things lives in **build mode (B)**.
Without the pusher's tools nothing is "picked": with no axe a tree says "you have no axe".
- **Pushing is Sokoban** (`life2/push.ts`): the thing moves at exactly your pace (it is drawn following your own step, `lfPropPos`, and only that step: `pr.followW`
  is the player's step count, so a box never follows a later step), and things in a line go together, any number of pieces (`PUSH_MAX` is effectively unlimited). Pushable: crates, barrels and stools; rocks
  (a pushed rock becomes a loose rock, `kind: 'rock'`, that pushes like a crate and is broken with the hammer for stone and now and then iron: build with them); and
  furniture in a room (chairs, beds, the family barrel, and the tile-only pieces: tables, counters, ovens, forges, anvils, looms and shelves, `PUSH_STATION` - they go out of doors as loose things and back in like the rest; the room's `seats`/`beds`/`barrel`/`spots.pantry` move with it, so `n.bed`/`n.seat` still point at it;
  refused when somebody is using it or when it would cut a seat, bed or the pantry off from the door). **Swap:** when the last piece cannot move you swap places with
  the piece you are holding, and again with the next, until you reach the wall. **People are pieces in the line**, just like boxes (pushed along, counted toward the five, swapped past at a wall); somebody kneeling or sitting on a bench blocks the line. **Sleepers and sitters ride along** (`pushCarry`): pushing a bed with somebody asleep in it (or a chair with a sitter) carries them with it, still asleep, and out of the door the bed becomes a loose thing with the sleeper as its `rider`; `n.bed`/`n.seat` are the same records so they follow. When a carried person comes to outside their house, `who.shoved` fires a ticker (shout, a grudge against the pusher, a chronicle line: "woke up in the middle of the forest, bed and all"). Nothing invisible stops a push: only what is drawn does (the tiles reserved round the stockpile yard and in front of stalls are not drawn, so they do not count).
  Loose things go over a field (whatever is growing or ploughed there goes back to bare dirt, `pushTrample`), through a doorway and into a house; standing in the doorway
  you swap with furniture that cannot move (it takes the doorway, you go in); furniture pushed onto the doorway becomes a loose thing (`pr.fur`, the room's record marked `out`) and is furniture
  again when pushed back onto the floor inside. A line is all loose things or all furniture. **Villagers move nothing for now** (`LF_NPC_MOVES_THINGS = false`: no
  tidying, rescuing or hauling; a villager shut in by a blocked door waits, and the chronicle says so after an hour).
- **Build mode** (`player/build-mode.ts`; **B** toggles, **I** opens the build menu): the build hotbar replaces the world hotbar (the hand always pushes: `itMode` is 'push'; no talking, searching, sleeping in tents or farming). **1** Push (default), **2** Destroy (`itHeld` is the hammer: smashes blocks, walls, doors, loose things, house furniture, lamp posts, tents and fires, and clears trees, rocks and crags to bare land),
  **3** Turn (`bldRotate`: loose chairs and beds use `pr.rot`, room ones `o.face`/`o.flip`), **4-9 and 0** are building items: pressing one puts one on the nearest free ground to the tile in front of you (`bldPlaceFront`; if the tile in front is taken by a loose thing the new one goes on the next free tile along the line, so a row can be pushed together; free for now, capped by `BUILD_CAP` loose things). In build mode **I** opens a build menu with only building items (`YARD_ITEMS`:
  wall, window wall, door, bed, chair, table, barrel, counter, oven, forge, anvil, loom, shelf, altar, pew, lamp post, tent, campfire): drag one onto a build slot (`itBuildAssign`). Lamp posts (`allLamps`), campfires (`fireList`, `allFires`) and tents are destroyable and buildable (a placed tent is decoration). The Builder's Yard in the square is gone (`LF_YARD = false`).
  Blocks and furniture are ordinary loose props and push, chain and swap like crates. Not destroyable yet (not nature): market stalls, the well, graves and the churchyard, bath pools, rugs, floors and paths.
- **Time stops in build mode** (`buildFrozen`/`updateFrozen` in `ai/decision-steps.ts`): the clock, villagers and economy wait; only you (walking, pushing, hammering, with action timers still running) and `bldTick` move. Villagers find out when time resumes (a carried sleeper wakes up; nobody knows who did it: no grudge, no name in the chronicle).
- **Sealing** (`life2/sealing.ts`, phase 2 of building): once a pushed block's step is over (`bldTick`, queued by `pushMoveProp` in `town.bldQ`), a flood (8-way, so walls touching only at a corner leak) from its neighbours looks for a closed region
  of at most `BLD_MAX` tiles with a door block (or a fixed door of a free room) beside it **and a way out** (`bldExit`: doors lead, through other rooms if need be, to open country; two rooms with a door only between them never seal, and a door added later seals the waiting neighbours too) and only grass, path, sand, crops, floor or berry and herb bushes inside (a tree, rock, water or the pad refuses; the bushes are cleared away when the room closes). It becomes a room: loose `bwall`/`bwindow`/`bdoor` blocks on its edge turn into
  fixed `T.WALL`/`T.DOOR` tiles, the inside is laid with `T.FLOOR` and `roomAt`, and a record is made (`free: true`, `kind: 'room'` so nobody lives in it yet, `cells` = inside tiles, `walls`, bounding box `x, y, w, h`, `doorX/doorY` = its first door; `noWindows`, never lit, no label).
  Thick walls: loose wall blocks lying against the new wall and held by two more blocks (a layer going round, corners included) become wall too; a lone line of blocks leading away stays loose. In build mode, hovering a tile shows what it is made of and what blocks it (`tileTip`), and the Islands list has an Export button (downloads the save as a file). In build mode hovering a bed shows whose it is and which room it is in, and hovering a villager shows their home, bed, needs and what they need next (`npcDebug`, a debugging view). **Fields:** the Field item (`field` in the build menu, default slot 0) is a brush, not a thing: switch it on and every grass tile you walk onto becomes a raw plot of your garden (`bldBrushStep`; `c.byField`; villagers take it up as farmers (`tutFarmers`), while the test-kit garden (`c.garden`) stays yours alone); you can plough and sow it too with the hoe; the hammer in build mode digs a plot back to grass. A partition block-line with a door inside a sealed room splits it (old record `dead`, new ones made); a lone block inside a room stays loose. A window block that sits in a straight wall with floor below becomes a window (`town.win`, also used by `tsWindow`).
  Free-form walls are drawn by their neighbours (`gaFreeTile` in render/village.ts: a face where floor lies just below, else a cap `cap_<open sides>`). Code written for rectangle rooms must use `cells`/`walls` for free ones (`r.free`); lighting, labels and room dressing skip them.
- **Demolition** (`bldBreak`, `bldDissolve` in life2/sealing.ts, phase 4): with the hammer in hand, walking into a wall built from blocks (`town.bldWall`) knocks that one block out; it just disappears. Every room it held up is taken apart (`bldDissolve`: floors go back to the ground they covered,
  `town.bldWas`; walls built from blocks that border no other sealed room become loose blocks again, pushable) and the ground is looked at again (`bldEvaluate`): a structure that is still closed re-seals (knock out a partition and two rooms become one), an open one stays loose, and a room left with no way out
  falls apart too (`bldExit`). Replace the missing block (push a wall block into the gap) to close it again. A wall shared with a standing room stays fixed. **The hammer is the delete button for what is built and pushed** (hammer in hand, walk into it): any wall or door (`bldHammerWall`; with the hammer in hand you cannot walk through a door), any loose thing (`bldDeleteProp`: blocks, crates, barrels, stools, beds, chairs, tables; rocks still break into stone; the yard stands stay)
  and the furniture standing in a house (`bldDeleteFurniture`). Hitting a wall or door of a room you built knocks out that one block (above). Hitting a wall or door of any village building (houses, church, tavern, shops, workshops, privies, the jail: no exceptions) deletes that tile and the whole building falls apart (`bldDemolish`): the floor goes back to grass,
  every other wall, window and door becomes a loose block, beds, chairs, tables and barrels become loose things (a barrel keeps the household's food), anything else inside is gone, whoever lived there is homeless (a tent goes up for each), `town.church`/`town.tavern` are cleared, and rooms of yours that leaned on its walls are looked at again. No repercussions yet: no gossip, crime, mood or rebuilding.
- **Nobody builds but you.** The village is pre-built at the start (`prebuildVillage`: houses, church, tavern, green, fields, privies, placed by `createSite` + `completeSite(site, true)`, instantly). There are no building projects, sites with materials, hauling or throwing wood onto rectangles,
  and no woodcutter or stonecutter jobs; unhoused villagers use tents and free beds (build rooms with beds and they move in: next phase). There is no carrying limit (`handsMax` is unlimited): felling a tree or breaking a rock puts wood or stone straight into the bag
  (`bagGet`, shown in the bag (I); the old "carried" chips in the bottom bar are gone). Costs for building blocks (planks, stone) are a later step.
- **Hotbar and inventory** (old belt, used when `IT_AUTO` is false): axe, hammer, hoe, rod, lockpicks, seeds on keys 1-9. In auto mode **I** opens the inventory: the hotbar (drag seeds and lockpicks onto it), the tools with wear (view only), seeds and lockpicks, goods and coin.
- In the old non-auto mode, with the wrong thing in hand you walk on over anything walkable (a field) and are told what to hold for anything solid. The hotbar's
  facing box always says what the next bump will do. After an action, keep holding to walk on, or let go and press again to act on the same thing.
- **For now the villager you steer is not troubled by hunger, bladder or tiredness** (`PLAYER_NEEDS = false` in `player/items.ts`: the values are pinned each tick, no forced bedtime; you can still choose to sleep in a bed). The test boxes round the square are off (`LF_PLAY_BOXES = false`); the Builder's Yard clears trees, rocks and loose things round itself (`yardClear`).
- The model: goods stay counts in `n.inv`; tools are single things with an owner and wear (`n.items`, `itGiveTool`); lockpicks and seeds are stacks in `n.bag`;
  `n.belt` holds 'hand', 'i<id>' or 's:<key>'; `n.sel` is the slot in hand, `n.mode` the hand mode. `itGate(n, tg, walkOn)` checks an action against the hand
  (`itNeedOf(key)`), `itFacing(n)` describes the thing in front. For testing (`IT.kit = 'test'`) whoever you steer starts with every tool, lockpicks, three kinds
  of seed and 100 denarii, plus a 3 x 2 garden by home.
- The villager you steer is given a dozen of every seed once (`itSeedGift`, also for saves made before a plant existed).
- **Crops and flowers** (`IT_SEEDS` in life2/items-trade.ts, art `CROP_ART`/`cropArt24` in art/painters.ts): barley (the wheat art), turnip, bean, potato, cabbage, tomato, corn, pumpkin, strawberry, carrot, each with its own plant in four stages (`crop_<seed>_1..4`, drawn by `drawCrops`), and five decorative flowers (lavender, poppy, daisy, marigold, rose: `flower: true`) that bloom and stay in bloom for `FLOWER_LIFE` (40 days), then wither (`itWilted`, sprite `_5`); bump a withered one with the hoe to clear it (a seed back) and plant again. Nobody eats or harvests flowers, farmers leave player-sown non-barley plots alone. Every seed has a bag stack `seed_<key>` and the Taberna sells them. The villager you steer walks every terrain at one pace (`tileCost` is ignored for `n.player`).
- Plots are drawn as `crop_raw` (untilled, weedy), `crop_0` (ploughed, dark furrows) and `crop_1..4` (growing, over the ploughed earth); they are `kind: 'tile'`
  sprites so they join without seams. A sown plot remembers its seed (`c.seed`); seed types growing differently is a later step.
- **Items in the economy** (`life2/items-trade.ts`): every working villager owns the tool for their work (`itNpcTool`; `n.toolQ` is its wear and is kept in step);
  a worn-out one is replaced at the smith or the Taberna (`itNpcNewTool`). Your tools wear with each use (`IT_WEAR`) and break. Breaking a rock sometimes gives
  iron ore (`itRockOre`: yours to sell to the smith; a stonecutter's goes to the smithy); the caravan still brings a little iron daily (a safety valve).
  The smith's counter offers each tool, buys ore, and sells lockpicks to friends (`itOffers`, `itExtras`). Seeds (`IT_SEEDS`): barley (staple), turnips (quick),
  beans; each gives its own food and a seed or two back (`itHarvest`); the Taberna sells seed; farmers leave the player's garden and sowings alone (`itFarmersPlot`).
  A lockpick in hand opens a house barred for the night (`itPickLock`: it may snap, somebody may hear; a break-in event). The Sawmill: a Miller (hired when food
  allows, sent to the fields when it is short) fells logs and saws them into planks (2 -> 3); planks are sold there and count as timber on a site or a wall.
- The other verbs in `lf2Verbs` (steal, wreck, shove, give, wake, follow ...) are still registered in the hooks but nothing reaches them yet.
  A new action means a key in `targetAt0` / `lifeAct` plus a line in `itNeedOf` saying what must be in hand.

## Islands and saves (src/ui/saves.ts)
Several islands are kept in the browser (IndexedDB `greenhollow`: `meta` rows for the list, `data` rows with the JSON). The game autosaves every ~30 s, when the tab hides and when you leave build mode, and boots into the last island (`gh.last`) or the Islands panel (button "Islands"). New island = Blank (one settler, a tent, nothing built: `blankStart` in `ai/decision-steps.ts`) or Ready village (the old pre-built start). There are no small/medium sizes. `isleCapture`/`isleRestore` encode the whole state graph (shared refs, Maps, Sets, typed arrays), skipping functions and `SAVE_SKIP` keys (plans are re-decided, `n.rand` is rebuilt). The Islands button (`#newTown`) only opens the list (a second handler once made a random new island and the autosave overwrote yours). Islands can be named (`ISLE.label`, shown in the list and the header; Rename on each row) and are deleted with an in-panel two-step button (no `confirm()`: the artifact sandbox blocks dialogs). Bump `SAVE_VERSION` when state changes shape; old saves can simply be deleted. Tests set `window.GH_NOSAVE`. Villager talk has speech bubbles as before, and the two who talk stand still for as long as the bubbles show (`talkUntil`, set in `drainEvents`), so a bubble never drifts away from its speaker; it is also logged in the Chatter tab (with a speech icon).

## Blank island: huts and visitors (src/life2/visitors.ts)
A sealed free room with a bed prop inside and a door becomes a hut (`visHomes`: `kind: 'house'`, `hut: true`, `r.beds` are the bed props themselves, `owner` on the prop; a bed pushed out is let go and its owner is homeless). `housingTick` then moves homeless people into free beds (it runs when you leave build mode, so you get the first bed at once, and when a visitor arrives; a bump into your own loose bed puts you to bed (`lifeAct`), loose beds do not block walking, `lfBedAt` in `world/construction.ts`, so owners can get into them and sleep there). `visitorTick` (hourly, blank scenario only): when a bed is free, a visitor comes ashore (`visArrive`, with a backstory in `n.back` put together at random from an ordinary moment and an unexplained arrival ("was ironing a shirt, and then there was sand"; `VIS_ORD`, `VIS_ARR`: they are confused, ask whether it is a dream, and accept it anyway), 6 grain of their own); the first arrival a day after the first free bed, then one every 3-5 days; from the third on the island needs food stock (`visFood() >= 8 + 6 * pop`). Singles first; couples once there are three people. Alone, `visitorMutter` has you think aloud about what to build next. A hut gets no kitchen hearth (`lf2PlaceHearth` would drop an oven tile in it that outlived the hut; old saves are cleaned by `clearOrphanOvens`). A privy seat (build item `toilet`) inside a built room makes `r.toilet` (a standing spot beside it, `r.ptoilet`; the room is called Privy if it has no bed) and `lfPrivies` offers it to villagers. You keep the same villager for good: `idleRelease` is off (`IDLE_RELEASE`), `setRole` never gives the steered villager a job, and the frame loop takes the reins back if anything lets go. The island label shows when it was last saved (click it to save now); a new seed makes a new island, never overwrites the current one. Villagers shout what they are missing when you are near (`visNeeds`: bed, privy, food; once per six hours each). Newcomers (`n.newcomer`, two days) shout `Land at last!`, walk up to the player and the others to introduce themselves (`visIntroTick`: a greeting, a welcome, a remark about the island) and keep remarking on the island (`VIS_ISLE`). Unowned beds are drawn grey (blanket index 5), owned ones keep their colour. There are no camp tents (`LF_TENTS = false`; the homeless bed down by the square). Walls and doors can only be broken in build mode. Barred doors at night are off (`LF2.feat.nightlocks = false`). Villagers do not leave the island (decided): the unhappy stay unhappy, and those who do not make it die there.

## The balance sheet and the tutorial (src/life2/needs.ts)
`townBalance()` measures the island in numbers: beds (people + 1 until `TUT_TARGET` = 10), privies (1 per `PRIVY_FOR` = 5), food (each person eats `FOOD_PER_HEAD` = 3.6 portions of grain a day, a plot gives `PLOT_YIELD` = 1.25 a day, 1.8 times that with a working baker, and a quarter of the food is fish and berries, so plots needed = ceil(people x 3.6 x 0.75 / yield)). `islandNeeds()` turns what is out of balance into requests in the order the island should grow: your bed, a second bed (one more than people), a privy, a field of the right size, the first harvest, beds for children, then bakery (3+ people and a harvest), smithy (4), tavern and shop (5), chapel (6), pews. The first request is shown big at the top of the Town tab with the balance table under it (`needsHTML`) and voiced by villagers near you. Hard requests (beds for children, a privy, a field, the harvest) stop new visitors (`tutBlocksArrival`), so the island only grows when it can feed and house itself; the bed requests do not, since a free bed is what brings a visitor. Villagers farm your field plots (`tutFarmers` turns gatherers into farmers when plots need work; the villager you steer is not counted in job caps; no builder or labourer jobs on a blank island). `town.tut.harvests` counts harvests. Not done yet: asking a villager "what do you need?" in conversation, mood falling while a request is ignored.

## Materials, the kit and the carpenter (src/life2/kit.ts, carpenter.ts)
On a blank island building uses a kit (`town.kit`, start `KIT_START`: 40 wall blocks, 3 doors, 2 window blocks, 3 beds, a privy seat, 2 chairs, a table, a shelf; the Field brush is free; the ready village is unlimited). Placing takes one (`kitTake` in `bldPlaceFront`), and everything you take down comes back in full (`kitGive`: hammer on a block, a loose thing, house furniture, a lamp, tent or fire), so nobody gets stuck. The build bar and menu show counts; an empty one says so (`kitOut`). More is made by the carpenter (`KIT_RECIPE`: wood for most things, stone for the hearth things). He comes ashore after the first harvest (`carpArrive`, `town.tut.carpenter`, 'was a ship's carpenter') with a carpentry table (`bench`, in your kit). A room with the bench and a shelf is the workshop (`FN_DEFS`), and the carpenter (`LF2_JOBS.Carpenter`, `carpPlan`) makes what `carpWant()` says the island lacks (beds for everybody, walls to keep 12, doors, a privy seat, the furniture of the next building wanted) as wood is in store, straight into your kit. Wood: everybody helps (the `Woodcutter` agenda, `wcPlan`, fells trees and stacks the wood at the workshop), and you hand wood and stone in by walking into the bench. The requests `carpenter`, `workshop` and `wood` say what is missing.

## On-screen request, signs and layout guides (src/ui/hud-needs.ts, life2/signs.ts, guides.ts)
Only the full-screen canvas is shown to a player, so the request lives on it: a pill at the top left ("● A privy", amber when it blocks visitors) that opens into a card with the request, how to meet it and the balance in a few lines (click it or press **N**; the choice is remembered in `gh.req`). Every room has a sign on the wall beside its door (`signWallOf`, `signMap`, drawn by `drawSigns`; walk into it, not in build mode, to hear `signText`: who lives there and free beds, or the purpose of a bakery/smithy/..., or, for an unfinished room, `signUnfinished`: "Unfinished bakery: oven ✓, counter ✗"). The first hut and the first privy come with a layout to follow (`GUIDE_DEFS`, `guidesUpdate` from the HUD tick, `drawGuides`): amber dashed squares for wall blocks, a blue one for the door, ghost furniture inside; they turn green as you fill them and vanish when the building exists. A guide only guides.

## Function rooms and what the island needs (src/life2/functions.ts, needs.ts)
A room you built takes its purpose from the furniture you placed in it (`fnScan`, hourly and when you leave build mode; nothing is added for you): oven + counter = bakery, forge + anvil = smithy, counter + shelf = shop (a loom inside gives the weaver's spot), counter + table + 2 chairs = tavern (`town.tavern`, chairs are `r.seats`), altar + 2 pews = chapel (`town.church`, pews are `r.pews`, a priest is chosen). The room gets that `kind`, a name and the standing spots the jobs use (`fnSpots`: oven, front, work, counter, bar, altar ...), found beside the furniture; a Baker, Smith or Shopkeeper is hired from whoever is free (`lf2AssignJobs`). Take the furniture out and the room goes back to a plain room (`fnRevert`). Such a room is not a home (`r.fn`). Loose chairs and pews are walked over like beds. `islandNeeds()` works out what is missing (beds, a privy, a field, food, bakery, smithy, shop, tavern, chapel, more pews, beds for children) with who says so, shown at the top of the Town tab (`needsHTML`) and voiced by villagers near you (`visNeeds`). With no larder merchants (a blank island) the baker collects what the households can spare from their pantries (`lf2PantryFetch`: more than three portions a head); bump the bakery's counter with grain in your bag to sell it (`sellgrain`), without grain to buy a loaf; the household's morning bread errand is never given to the villager you steer. Not done yet: couples' beds joining up, a real "ask for a bed" request, the old shopkeepers' jobs after demolition.

## Magic mushrooms (src/life2/shrooms.ts)
About 40 magic mushrooms are scattered when an island is made (`shroomScatter`, `town.shrooms`, mostly at the edge of the trees, the same for the same seed); one more may grow each morning (`shroomGrow`, cap `SH_CAP`; built-over ones vanish). Walking into one (not in build mode) eats it (`eatShroom`): `game.trip` starts for `SH_TRIP` game minutes (a few hours; more mushrooms extend it and deepen it). `psyLevel()` (0..~1.3) rises over 25 minutes and fades over the last 50. While it lasts `psyPost` (called at the end of `render`) bends the finished frame: horizontal strips wave, the colours cycle and oversaturate, the last frame echoes, the view breathes, a coloured rim pulses and sparks orbit you; mushrooms glow in rainbow colours; you think odd thoughts (`SH_THOUGHTS`) and a villager nearby may remark on it (`SH_NPC`). Sprite `magic_shroom` (painters.ts). Old saves get their mushrooms on load.

## Your own heart is yours (src/life/aging-romance.ts, ui/hud-needs.ts)
Nobody courts or marries the villager you steer unless you say so. When the village's romance would pair someone with you, `askCourtship` / `askMarriage` set `town.decision` instead: a card in the middle of the screen (Yes / Not yet / No) and **time stands still until you answer** (`answerDecision`; the frame loop skips the sim while `town.decision` is set). Yes starts a courtship (`startCourtship`) or schedules the wedding; Not yet asks again in 6 days (courting) or 8 (marrying); No ends it, costs some affection and is remembered (`refusedAt`, 20 days). Villagers' own romances are unchanged. Tides are gone: `tideLevel()` stands at its middle level and the tide talk and legends are removed. Every privy seat in a closed room with a free tile beside it is a privy (`r.pseats`, `lfPrivies`); seats outside a room are counted as loose (`looseSeats`) and explained in the request. TrueSight adds the points where a wall crosses the edge of the view (`tsPolygon`), so nothing visible is left in shadow when zoomed in.

## Voice and places (Murakami; src/dialogue/place-words.ts, tools/blankwords.mjs)
The island's text is written in the voice of *The Elephant Vanishes*: ordinary people calmly accepting an impossible situation (an ordinary moment, then sand), dry, specific, understated, no exclamation marks, numbers kept exact. That covers arrivals, the requests (`islandNeeds`), shouts, thoughts, signs, chronicle lines and hints. On a blank island nobody talks about places that are not there: `placeWords` (called from `cvSpeak`, `chron`, `goS`, `waitS`) turns the old village's words into what exists (tavern -> the camp fire unless a tavern is built, chapel/church -> the clearing unless built, square -> the clearing, street/lane/road -> the path, the well -> the shore, market/stalls -> the beach, town/village -> island); nobody takes holy orders before a chapel exists. `node tools/blankwords.mjs [days] [people]` runs a blank island with a hut and arrivals, records every spoken, shouted and chronicled line plus conversations, and lists lines that mention a place that may not be there. The generic conversation banks are still the village's: read samples with `node tools/talk.mjs`.

## Conversation (src/dialogue/cv-*.ts)
Talking is generated from the village, not from a menu. You say whole sentences; the attitude (a joke, sympathy, an insult, a probe,
a flirt, gossip, a lie) is part of the sentence, and the person answers the attitude as well as the words. There is no tone selector and no Goodbye reply (a small Leave control instead).
- `cv-world.ts`: the village behind the talk. `cvLife(o)` (ambition, regret, pride, fear, maybe a secret), `cvWorld()` (old stories tying people together: debts, feuds, old flames,
  who knows what; legends), `cvMem(o,p)` (what they remember of you: trust, moments with your own words), `cvKnows(p)` (your journal), `cvSaw` (harmless sightings, e.g. you pushed someone).
  Generated lazily per town seed, so a seed gives the same village; it also nudges the sim (debtors repay, feuds sour opinions).
- `cv-voice.ts`: nine ways of talking (`cvVoice`: talkative, reserved, nosy, cynic, sunny, wary, prickly, smooth, lonely) and their banks (greetings, reactions, deflections, gossip lead-ins).
- `cv-topics.ts`: `cvPool(c)` = everything they might bring up (events they know, people, old stories, their own life, legends, the day, the tide, shops, the chronicle, things about you), weighted;
  `cvWilling` = how much they will tell (trust, who they are, what you have done this talk).
- `cv-tell.ts`: `cvTell(c, topic, layer)` turns a topic into words, layer by layer (0 a hint, 1 the story, 2-3 what they do not usually say). Every layer names things you can follow up.
- `cv-talk.ts`: options (`cvFollowUps`, `cvObserve`, `cvShare`, `cvSpecial`; each option has an `intent`), `cvEffect` (what each attitude does to opinion, trust, patience, events, memory),
  `cvRespond` / `cvDeepen` / `cvPivotUtt` (go deeper, wander to a related topic, they ask you something), `cvClose` (they have somewhere to be; walk with them), `convBegin` (entry point).
- Authored scenes (`dialogue/opinions-scenes.ts`) still fire first when they apply; after a scene reply the conversation carries on here (`cvAfterScene`).
- To add content: a bit of activity talk in `CV_ACTV`, a thing on their mind in `CV_STATES`, a legend in `CV_LEGENDS`, a secret/regret/fear in `cv-world.ts`, new option lines in `cvFollowUps`.
  Avoid generic filler ("That is interesting", "It is complicated", "Anything else?"): `CV_BANNED` and the tests reject it.
- `node tools/talk.mjs [seed] [people] [turns] [random|first]` prints real conversations; read them after every content change.
- `node tools/longevity.mjs [days] [talks/day] [turns] [seed] [--file=other.html]` measures freshness (new templates, new facts per day) and readability over many days; `node tools/sift.mjs [days]` counts story-shaped patterns the village produces on its own. Findings are in NOTES.md.
- Speak events through `cvMe(o, sbText(o, ev))`, not `ev.text`: it gives the speaker's own version and turns their name into I/me and the player's into you.
- `cv-rules.ts`: lines chosen by the facts of the moment (most specific rule wins) with fill-ins; every line is chosen by what the *listener* heard least recently.
- `cv-speech.ts`: `cvSpeak(text)` contracts speech on the way out ("do not" -> "don't", "is it not" -> "isn't it"; not at a clause end, not "I have a ...").
  Every speech surface calls it (talk box and options in `renderTalk`, bubbles and Overheard in `drainEvents`, `playerSay`). Banks may be written either way.
- `cv-answers.ts`: every follow-up option has its own `then` answer (what somebody is like, how they are taking it, how you know, how it started ...);
  an option without one only deepens its subject. Ask-about (`cvAskAbout`): people, news, old stories and places the player knows; "never heard of it"
  gives a `tellit` hook so you can tell them. New follow-ups need an answer function, not a fall-through.
- Never name the speaker or the player in the third person in speech: run event/chronicle text through `cvSpeechOf(o, text, listener)` (first names,
  no clock times, the speaker as I, the listener as you).
- `node tools/lintalk.mjs [days] [talks] [turns] [seed]` flags what reads wrong (self/player in the third person, repeats, clunky pivots, infants as topics,
  full names, clock times, child lines from adults, uncontracted speech, broken grammar, news told about the listener, repeated overheard pairs) and prints
  question -> answer pairs to read. A test runs it.

## The social simulation (src/social2/sb-*.ts; design in docs/social-simulation.md)
The village lives without the player: the simulation owns the truth (events), each villager owns what they believe, conversation expresses it.
- `sb-core.ts`: each person's **version** of an event (`o.kn[id]`: `who` they blame, `ext` extra detail, `sev2`, `m` times changed, `hop`, `sus` = only a suspicion),
  `sbKind` (own / fact / hearsay / rumour), importance and forgetting (`sbImp`, `sbTrim`), distortion when retold (`sbPassOn`: exaggerate, soften for a friend, pin it on someone
  they dislike, lose the name), second-order beliefs (`o.sbRel['a:b']` = what they think a feels about b, and why), the reason ledger (`o.why[id]`, written by `lfAff(a, b, v, why)`),
  trust and respect (`o.trust`, `o.resp`), `sbWhyText` (a reason in the speaker's words).
- `sb-exchange.ts`: background exchanges between people who are near each other and free (`SBX`: small talk, news, vent, praise, console, shop/rivalry, family/row,
  argue, court, borrow, repay, confide, leak). Each has consequences; text only appears if the player is near. `sbCompat` is a pair's chemistry. Loans live in `town.sbLoans`.
- `sb-voice.ts`: the words. `sbSayIfNear(a, b, kind, facts)` voices an exchange with its facts (who, why, how much, what the row is about); `sbNewsLines` is gossip
  in the teller's version; `sbSmallTalk(a, b)` is what people say when they bump into each other (also `makeConvo`), drawn from their lives (family by name,
  where somebody is, shared news, work, prices, the tide). `sbPick` chooses what the player has heard least recently.
- `sb-life.ts`: life events once a day (illness and sick visits, lost and found coins, pilfering with a suspect, windfalls, catches, a business week, deliveries),
  ill people in bed, parents fetching food for a hungry child.
- `sb-presim.ts`: 60 days of abstract village life before arrival (meals, work, the street, the tavern), seeded by the town seed, money-neutral, ~25 ms.
- Conversation side: `dialogue/cv-social.ts` (hedges, `cvMe`/`cvYou` first-person rewriting, "who cannot stand whom" and "today I talked to…" topics,
  contradicting a version with "I heard it was X").
- Measure with `node tools/network.mjs [days] [seed]` (the village at arrival, how news travels, versions, wrong blame) and `node tools/sift.mjs [days]`.

## Tests
- `tests/lib.mjs` loads `dist/index.html` deterministically: seeded `Math.random`, a fake clock, frame loop
  held, the harness steps the sim (`simulate(page, minutes)`) the way the frame loop does. Same seed, same result.
- Current suite: load and run a day, render without errors, nobody starves in 12 days (three towns),
  the controls (Q cycles the hand modes, 1-9 the belt, Shift and old keys do nothing; Push pushes people and swaps against a wall, Talk talks; crates pushed in Push (Sokoban: at your pace, in lines of three, swapping at a wall, people as pieces of the line, no invisible walls, over fields, through doors; furniture out and back in; villagers move nothing) and searched in Search; rocks pushed and broken; chairs, beds and the barrel moved; trees need the axe; the hoe ploughs and seeds sow; sleepers), conversations (specific openers, long random talks without dead ends or repeats, insults and sympathy, unlocking layers, memory, witnesses, the arrest), sim speed under 3 s per game-day (about 0.7 s now),
  the social simulation (the pre-simulation is deterministic, fast and leaves nobody a blank slate; news spreads gradually and changes; talk uses beliefs and you can contradict a version),
  readability and a week of freshness, items in the economy (villager tools, wear and the smith, iron, seeds and the garden, lockpicks, the sawmill), the dialogue lint, every follow-up answered and ask-about, overheard small talk from real lives (87 tests, about 4 minutes).
- The random stream is shared, so any change shifts every later random draw: a test that suddenly fails in one town is often exposing a real
  weakness that luck was hiding (that is how children starving with dole grain in their pockets was found). Find the cause before changing the test.
- When behaviour changes on purpose, tests may need new expectations; when it changes by accident, they catch it.

## What the player can see: TrueSight (src/render/truesight.ts)
Visibility, not lighting (night, lamps and shadows stay in render/lighting-wild.ts). Buildings are polygons whose walls are as thick as they are drawn:
every wall tile is a solid square and the segments are the straight edges of the solid parts, merged along each wall. So a building's outer corner casts
from the outermost point of its corner tile, a doorway is a real opening between two jambs (the beam through it starts at the inside corners of the door
frame), and a window is a gap in the middle of its tile with a solid piece either side.
From the player's eye a ray goes to every segment end (and a hair either side), stopping at the first segment; the hit points in angle order are the
visibility polygon. Everything outside it is covered in near-black, cut along those straight lines. Only what a sight line reaches is shown: the wall
behind each visible stretch of face (`tsFaces`: a strip one tile deep), so a glancing view shows a sliver; no tile is ever lit as a whole (doors included).
- ONLY buildings block sight: walls, closed doors, the solid bits beside a window. Trees, rocks, furniture, crates and people never do.
- At an inner corner (the face ends at the segment's own end and solid wall carries on round it) `tsFaces` shows the corner tile too, so the four outer corner tiles of a room are not black notches when you stand inside.
- Only one plate of a hole casts at a time: from above (eye north of the tile) the light is cast from the bottom plate, from below from the top plate (`pf`/`pty` flags in `tsGeo`, honoured by `tsRay`). `tsLab` has a wall of test holes (`town.tsHoles`, kinds in `TS_HOLES`: slit, narrow, wide, low, high, archway, window) to compare shapes.
- Windows have their upright dividers (`bars`) as thin solids in both plates, so each pane casts its own beam and the divider casts a dark line; the horizontal divider lies along the line of sight and casts nothing from above.
- Side windows: a window block in a wall that runs up and down (floor to its east or west) becomes a window in a side wall (`town.winS`, drawn as the `window_side` overlay on the wall top); in TrueSight its plates are upright (`tsHoleRot`, `TS_HOLES.windowSide`). The closed sides of a hole tile are blockers only (`pf` 3) and never light a face.
- A window is a hole in a wall tile (`TS_HOLES`, fractions of the tile, taken from the glass of the sprite): two thin plates, solid but for the hole, at its top and bottom edges, so sight passes only through the hole and the lit strip of a window plate stops at its own tile. A new hole shape is a new entry.
- A window tile shows its whole face wherever its outer line can be seen (`tsFaces`), so the wall beside it never shades the tile; only the glass (the `TS_HOLES` rectangle) follows the sight line through it, lit where you can see through and dark where you cannot.
- `tsGeo()` builds (and caches, keyed by `town.gridV` and door states) the segments in a cell grid; `tsRay` finds the nearest hit; `tsPolygon`, `tsWalls`.
  `tsSees(x, y)` = can the player see a person standing there (`canSee` uses it; hidden people are not drawn, picked or named).
  `tsLine(ax, ay, bx, by)` = a clear line on the same segments, for NPC perception with its own rules (NPCs still use the older `seeThrough`).
- Public buildings keep their doors open by day (`tsPropDoors`). Zoomed out to the map (zoom < 0.6) nothing is hidden.
- `node tools/truesight.mjs [--out=dir]` stamps a test ground (`tsLab`: rooms with an archway and a shut door, a corridor with three corners, a house
  with windows, trees, a shed, walls touching corner to corner), checks who is seen from each spot (and while people walk), and saves screenshots. Tests run it.
- What you cannot see is `TS_SHADE` (70%) black in the place you are in and `TS_DARK` (90%) black elsewhere and inside buildings (`TS_SHADE`) in the place you are in, and fully dark elsewhere: out of doors the open ground is half dark and buildings stay black; inside a room (`roomAt`) that room and its walls are half dark and the whole outside goes really dark.
- **Villager sight** (`drawSightCones`, `sightPoly`, V toggles, `SIGHT_VIS`): each villager sees a cone (`SIGHT_RANGE` 7 tiles, `SIGHT_HALF` either side of `n.face`) cut by the same building geometry, drawn light red on the ground. A debugging view for stealth; not yet used by NPC perception.
- ~0.2 ms per recompute at normal zoom, ~0.5 ms zoomed out, only when the eye, the view or a door changes.

## Levels: stairs, roofs and the earth below (src/life2/stairs.ts, growLevels in world/construction.ts)
The map is stacked downwards in blocks of `town.h0` rows (`LV_N` = 5, `lvlOf(y)`): block 0 the ground, 1 and 2 the levels above it (roof decks), 3 and 4 the earth below (mines). It is ordinary map, so building, sealing, props, rooms, walking, TrueSight and NPC sight work on every level unchanged, only the coordinates differ (`growLevels`: saves made before get the levels on load; ground things pick places among `GH()` rows only, so the random stream is unchanged; `town.ore` is 0 none / 1 iron / 2 copper / 3 gold, laid in veins, richer in the second mine). Tiles: `T.SKY` (nothing, transparent), `T.DECK`, `T.STAIR`/`T.STAIRD` (up, and the way down from the deck), `T.EARTH` (solid, dig it), `T.DIRTF` (dug-out floor), `T.STAIRM` (a stair down, on the ground) / `T.STAIRU` (its way up, in the mine). `bldBare(y)` is what a removed wall leaves (grass, deck or dirt).
- **Stairs up** (build item `stair`, kit 1, 6 wood): in build mode stand on a floor tile of a room facing a clear floor tile; the stair goes in front of you and you stand on its landing. A deck is laid over the whole footprint of that building one level up (`stairFootprint`; neighbouring decks join) and you can build on it; a room up there takes stairs to the third level.
- **Stairs down** (`stairdown`): on open ground (or deep in a mine) a stair goes into the earth: a 3 x 3 chamber of dug-out floor, surrounded by solid earth. Bump the stair to use it. Digging: the hammer on `T.EARTH` (`digAction`, key `dig..`, needs the hammer) clears it; ore gives iron, copper or gold (`n.inv`), now and then stone; the map's edge and the bottom rows are solid. Underground you can build rooms (sealing accepts `T.DIRTF`) or just dig out houses.
- **One way in**: every stair tile has an arrow (`stairRot`, sprites `stair_up`/`stair_down` turned 0-3, `stairSpriteId`) pointing from its landing (the tile you stood on to place it) into the stair. You use a stair only by walking into it from its landing (`stairAction`; from anywhere else it just says so), and it is a straight cut: you appear at once on the landing at the other end, where the stair back down stands *behind* you (`stairMake`: the far stair is on the far side of its landing), so holding the key walks you away from it. A landing serves one staircase only (placement refuses a landing that is already one).
- `town.portals` maps each landing to its partner, `town.stairs` lists the pairs `{ s0, l0, s1, l1, kind }`; `findPath` and `refreshReach` follow the portals (cost 3), `stepNpc` teleports at a non-adjacent step (`teleportTo`), so villagers use stairs like anybody. Up on a roof the level below is drawn dimmed underneath (`render`), and a deck hides what is under it; mines are drawn as dirt with ore flecks, lit only by what TrueSight shows (earth is solid to sight).
- **Huts on other levels**: a hut (sealed room with a bed and a door) upstairs or underground is a home like any other (`housingTick` looks at every free bed; a flood that reaches a staircase landing counts as a way out in `bldFlood`), and villagers walk there by the stairs. `levelWord(y)` / `placeOf(n)` say where somebody is ("upstairs", "the roof", "the mine"), shown in `npcDebug` (build mode hover).
- **Torches** (build item `torch`, kit 8, 1 wood makes 3): a loose prop (`kind: 'torch'`, art `torch`). Underground the day and night lighting is replaced by darkness (`drawMineDark` in render/truesight.ts) with a small glow round you (`MINE.own`) and a wider warm one round every torch (`MINE.torch`); TrueSight still decides what a sight line reaches.
- Not done yet: taking down the building below should take its deck with it, hammering a stair, villagers' beds and jobs upstairs and underground, ore being used (the smith buys iron ore as before; copper and gold only sell), mine lighting and air, cave-ins.

## Performance tools
- `node tools/profile.mjs <seconds> <zoom> [--pan] [--cpu] [--file=other.html]`: the real frame loop, with per-stage times, frame-time percentiles and optional panning / CPU profile. Headless Chromium paints in software, so compare before/after rather than against 60 fps.
- `node tools/diff.mjs a.html b.html [--no-people]`: renders both builds at fixed views and compares pixels. Use it after any rendering change.
- Rendering rules learned: villagers are cached as sprites when zoomed out (`ART.drawDollLOD`); the lit-area texture is rebuilt at 20 Hz; the tide tile list is cached; do not skip the island overview image in `drawGround` (it shows through at close zoom).
