# Greenhollow

A Roman village simulation played in the browser: villagers with needs, jobs, families, gossip,
crime and a town watch, drawn on a canvas. The owner is hands-off with GitHub: do the git work
(branches, commits, pushes, merging to main when tests pass) yourself.

## Commands
- `npm run build`: joins `src/` into `dist/index.html`, the single self-contained file that is played and published.
- `npm run typecheck`: TypeScript check (`tsc --noEmit`). Must stay at zero errors.
- `npm test`: build, typecheck, then the headless-browser tests in `tests/run.mjs` (about 2 minutes).
  `node tests/run.mjs food` runs only tests whose name contains "food".
- After changing `src/`, run `npm run build` and commit `dist/index.html` with the change (CI checks it is up to date).

## How the code is put together (read before editing)
- `src/*/*.ts` are **classic scripts sharing one global scope**, not ES modules. `scripts/build.mjs`
  concatenates them in the order of `src/order.json`, strips types with esbuild and inlines them with
  `src/styles.css` into `src/index.html`. A new file must be added to `src/order.json` (the build fails otherwise).
- Order matters: later files patch earlier ones, e.g. `life2/glue.ts` wraps `lifeHud`
  (`const _hud = lifeHud; lifeHud = function ...`). Those spots carry `@ts-expect-error -- deliberate patch`.
- Function declarations are hoisted across the whole joined script, so if a name is declared twice the
  **last** one wins everywhere. Don't declare a function twice; replace it in place.
- `art/painters.ts` is a sealed IIFE (`const ART = ...`) holding all sprite and tile painting; the game reaches it only through `ART.*`.
- Folders: `world/` generation and island, `life/` and `life2/` village life (life2 = the Roman layer: events,
  gossip, trades, law, daily life, grudges), `ai/` villager plans and decisions, `social/` village
  social life, `talk/` the new conversation engine (in progress), `ceremonies/`, `player/` controlling a villager, `render/` drawing, `ui/` panels, input and the main loop.
- Types are loose for now (`strict: false`, many `: any` from the JS conversion). Tighten gradually: prefer
  adding real interfaces (Person, Town, Game) over more `any`.

## Controls (src/player/items.ts, src/life2/controls.ts)
**Auto controls** (`IT_AUTO = true` in `player/items.ts`; set it false to get the old hand modes and belt tools back): in the world a bump does the obvious thing: talk to a villager (or buy from a seller), search a crate or barrel (once a day), fell a tree (axe), break a rock (hammer), fish (rod), work a plot (hoe, and the seed you have most of, or the one in hand).
The tool is picked for you (`itFits` checks you own it; `itWearTool` wears it; the tool strip beside the hotbar shows wear and lights the one about to be used). The hotbar has **ten** slots (1-0) and holds only seeds and lockpicks (`n.belt`, drag them in from the inventory **I**; pressing the held slot puts it away; lockpicks must be held to pick a lock). Q does nothing; Shift and the old keys do nothing.
**Field rule:** a plot that needs work is worked only when you start from rest beside it (`p.fresh`, set by a press after a quarter-second of rest, cleared by stepping onto anything that is not a crop tile), so walking over a field on your way somewhere never ploughs it, and working a row keeps going as you hold the key. Pushing, smashing and turning things lives in **build mode (B)**.
Without the pusher's tools nothing is "picked": with no axe a tree says "you have no axe".
- **Pushing is Sokoban** (`life2/push.ts`): the thing moves at exactly your pace (it is drawn following your own step, `lfPropPos`, and only that step: `pr.followW`
  is the player's step count, so a box never follows a later step), and things in a line go together, any number of pieces (`PUSH_MAX` is effectively unlimited). Pushable: crates, barrels and stools; rocks
  (a pushed rock becomes a loose rock, `kind: 'rock'`, that pushes like a crate and is broken with the hammer for stone and now and then iron: build with them); and
  furniture in a room (chairs, beds, the family barrel, and the tile-only pieces: tables, counters, ovens, forges, anvils, looms and shelves, `PUSH_STATION` - they go out of doors as loose things and back in like the rest; the room's `seats`/`beds`/`barrel`/`spots.pantry` move with it, so `n.bed`/`n.seat` still point at it;
  refused when somebody is using it or when it would cut a seat, bed or the pantry off from the door). **Swap:** when the last piece cannot move you swap places with
  the piece you are holding, and again with the next, until you reach the wall. **People are pieces in the line**, just like boxes (pushed along, counted toward the five, swapped past at a wall); somebody kneeling or sitting on a bench blocks the line. **Sleepers and sitters ride along** (`pushCarry`): pushing a bed with somebody asleep in it (or a chair with a sitter) carries them with it, still asleep, and out of the door the bed becomes a loose thing with the sleeper as its `rider`; `n.bed`/`n.seat` are the same records so they follow. When a carried person comes to outside their house, `who.shoved` fires a ticker (shout, a grudge against the pusher, a chronicle line: "woke up in the middle of the forest, bed and all"). Nothing invisible stops a push: only what is drawn does (the tiles reserved round the stockpile yard and in front of stalls are not drawn, so they do not count).
  Loose things go over a field (whatever is growing or ploughed there goes back to bare dirt, `pushTrample`), through a doorway and into a house; standing in the doorway
  you swap with furniture that cannot move (it takes the doorway, you go in); furniture pushed onto the doorway becomes a loose thing (`pr.fur`, the room's record marked `out`) and is furniture
  again when pushed back onto the floor inside. A line is all loose things or all furniture. **Villagers move nothing for now** (`LF_NPC_MOVES_THINGS = false`: no
  tidying, rescuing or hauling; a villager shut in by a blocked door waits, and the chronicle says so after an hour).
- **Build mode** (`player/build-mode.ts`; **B** toggles, **I** opens the build menu): the build hotbar replaces the world hotbar (the hand always pushes: `itMode` is 'push'; no talking, searching, sleeping in tents or farming). **1** Push (default), **2** Destroy (`itHeld` is the hammer: smashes blocks, walls, doors, loose things, house furniture, lamp posts, tents and fires, and clears trees, rocks and crags to bare land),
  **3** Turn (`bldRotate`: loose chairs and beds use `pr.rot`, room ones `o.face`/`o.flip`), **4-9 and 0** are building items: pressing one puts one on the nearest free ground to the tile in front of you (`bldPlaceFront`; if the tile in front is taken by a loose thing the new one goes on the next free tile along the line, so a row can be pushed together; free for now, capped by `BUILD_CAP` loose things). In build mode **I** opens a build menu with only building items (`YARD_ITEMS`:
  wall, window wall, door, bed, chair, table, barrel, counter, oven, forge, anvil, loom, shelf, altar, pew, lamp post, tent, campfire): drag one onto a build slot (`itBuildAssign`). Lamp posts (`allLamps`), campfires (`fireList`, `allFires`) and tents are destroyable and buildable (a placed tent is decoration). The Builder's Yard in the square is gone (`LF_YARD = false`).
  Blocks and furniture are ordinary loose props and push, chain and swap like crates. Not destroyable yet (not nature): market stalls, the well, graves and the churchyard, bath pools, rugs, floors and paths.
- **Time stops in build mode** (`buildFrozen`/`updateFrozen` in `ai/decision-steps.ts`): the clock, villagers and economy wait; only you (walking, pushing, hammering, with action timers still running) and `bldTick` move. Villagers find out when time resumes (a carried sleeper wakes up; nobody knows who did it: no grudge, no name in the chronicle).
- **Sealing** (`life2/sealing.ts`, phase 2 of building): once a pushed block's step is over (`bldTick`, queued by `pushMoveProp` in `town.bldQ`), a flood (8-way, so walls touching only at a corner leak) from its neighbours looks for a closed region
  of at most `BLD_MAX` tiles with a door block (or a fixed door of a free room) beside it **and a way out** (`bldExit`: doors lead, through other rooms if need be, to open country; two rooms with a door only between them never seal, and a door added later seals the waiting neighbours too) and only grass, path, sand, crops, floor or berry and herb bushes inside (a tree, rock, water or the pad refuses; the bushes are cleared away when the room closes). It becomes a room: loose `bwall`/`bwindow`/`bdoor` blocks on its edge turn into
  fixed `T.WALL`/`T.DOOR` tiles, the inside is laid with `T.FLOOR` and `roomAt`, and a record is made (`free: true`, `kind: 'room'` so nobody lives in it yet, `cells` = inside tiles, `walls`, bounding box `x, y, w, h`, `doorX/doorY` = its first door; `noWindows`, never lit, no label).
  Thick walls: loose wall blocks lying against the new wall and held by two more blocks (a layer going round, corners included) become wall too; a lone line of blocks leading away stays loose. In build mode, hovering a tile shows what it is made of and what blocks it (`tileTip`), and the Islands list has an Export button (downloads the save as a file). In build mode hovering a bed shows whose it is and which room it is in, and hovering a villager shows their home, bed, needs and what they need next (`npcDebug`, a debugging view). **Fields:** the Field item (`field` in the build menu, default slot 0) is a brush, not a thing: switch it on and every grass tile you walk onto becomes a raw plot of your garden (`bldBrushStep`; `c.byField`; villagers take it up as farmers (`tutFarmers`), while the test-kit garden (`c.garden`) stays yours alone); you can plough and sow it too with the hoe; the hammer in build mode digs a plot back to grass. A partition block-line with a door inside a sealed room splits it (old record `dead`, new ones made); a lone block inside a room stays loose. A window block that sits in a straight wall with floor below becomes a window (`town.win`, also used by `tsWindow`).
  Free-form walls are drawn by their neighbours (`gaFreeTile` in render/village.ts: a face where floor lies just below, else a cap `cap_<open sides>`). Code written for rectangle rooms must use `cells`/`walls` for free ones (`r.free`); lighting, labels and room dressing skip them.
- **Demolition** (`bldBreak`, `bldDissolve` in life2/sealing.ts, phase 4): with the hammer in hand, walking into a wall built from blocks (`town.bldWall`) knocks that one block out; it just disappears. Every room it held up is taken apart (`bldDissolve`: floors go back to the ground they covered,
  `town.bldWas`; walls built from blocks that border no other sealed room become loose blocks again, pushable) and the ground is looked at again (`bldEvaluate`): a structure that is still closed re-seals (knock out a partition and two rooms become one), an open one stays loose, and a room left with no way out
  falls apart too (`bldExit`). Replace the missing block (push a wall block into the gap) to close it again. A wall shared with a standing room stays fixed. **The hammer is the delete button for what is built and pushed** (hammer in hand, walk into it): any wall or door (`bldHammerWall`; with the hammer in hand you cannot walk through a door), any loose thing (`bldDeleteProp`: blocks, crates, barrels, stools, beds, chairs, tables; rocks still break into stone; the yard stands stay)
  and the furniture standing in a house (`bldDeleteFurniture`). Hitting a wall or door of a room you built knocks out that one block (above). Hitting a wall or door of any village building (houses, church, tavern, shops, workshops, privies, the jail: no exceptions) deletes that tile and the whole building falls apart (`bldDemolish`): the floor goes back to grass,
  every other wall, window and door becomes a loose block, beds, chairs, tables and barrels become loose things (a barrel keeps the household's food), anything else inside is gone, whoever lived there is homeless (a tent goes up for each), `town.church`/`town.tavern` are cleared, and rooms of yours that leaned on its walls are looked at again. No repercussions yet: no gossip, crime, mood or rebuilding.
- **Nobody builds but you.** The village is pre-built at the start (`prebuildVillage`: houses, church, tavern, green, fields, privies, placed by `createSite` + `completeSite(site, true)`, instantly). There are no building projects, sites with materials, hauling or throwing wood onto rectangles,
  and no woodcutter or stonecutter jobs; unhoused villagers use tents and free beds (build rooms with beds and they move in: next phase). There is no carrying limit (`handsMax` is unlimited): felling a tree or breaking a rock puts wood or stone straight into the bag
  (`bagGet`, shown in the bag (I); the old "carried" chips in the bottom bar are gone). Costs for building blocks (planks, stone) are a later step.
- **Hotbar and inventory** (old belt, used when `IT_AUTO` is false): axe, hammer, hoe, rod, lockpicks, seeds on keys 1-9. In auto mode **I** opens the inventory: the hotbar (drag seeds and lockpicks onto it), the tools with wear (view only), seeds and lockpicks, goods and coin.
- In the old non-auto mode, with the wrong thing in hand you walk on over anything walkable (a field) and are told what to hold for anything solid. The hotbar's
  facing box always says what the next bump will do. After an action, keep holding to walk on, or let go and press again to act on the same thing.
- **For now the villager you steer is not troubled by hunger, bladder or tiredness** (`PLAYER_NEEDS = false` in `player/items.ts`: the values are pinned each tick, no forced bedtime; you can still choose to sleep in a bed). The test boxes round the square are off (`LF_PLAY_BOXES = false`); the Builder's Yard clears trees, rocks and loose things round itself (`yardClear`).
- The model: goods stay counts in `n.inv`; tools are single things with an owner and wear (`n.items`, `itGiveTool`); lockpicks and seeds are stacks in `n.bag`;
  `n.belt` holds 'hand', 'i<id>' or 's:<key>'; `n.sel` is the slot in hand, `n.mode` the hand mode. `itGate(n, tg, walkOn)` checks an action against the hand
  (`itNeedOf(key)`), `itFacing(n)` describes the thing in front. For testing (`IT.kit = 'test'`) whoever you steer starts with every tool, lockpicks, three kinds
  of seed and 100 denarii, plus a 3 x 2 garden by home.
- The villager you steer is given a dozen of every seed once (`itSeedGift`, also for saves made before a plant existed).
- **Crops and flowers** (`IT_SEEDS` in life2/items-trade.ts, art `CROP_ART`/`cropArt24` in art/painters.ts): barley (the wheat art), turnip, bean, potato, cabbage, tomato, corn, pumpkin, strawberry, carrot, each with its own plant in four stages (`crop_<seed>_1..4`, drawn by `drawCrops`), and five decorative flowers (lavender, poppy, daisy, marigold, rose: `flower: true`) that bloom and stay in bloom for `FLOWER_LIFE` (40 days), then wither (`itWilted`, sprite `_5`); bump a withered one with the hoe to clear it (a seed back) and plant again. Nobody eats or harvests flowers, farmers leave player-sown non-barley plots alone. Every seed has a bag stack `seed_<key>` and the Taberna sells them. The villager you steer walks every terrain at one pace (`tileCost` is ignored for `n.player`).
- Plots are drawn as `crop_raw` (untilled, weedy), `crop_0` (ploughed, dark furrows) and `crop_1..4` (growing, over the ploughed earth); they are `kind: 'tile'`
  sprites so they join without seams. A sown plot remembers its seed (`c.seed`); seed types growing differently is a later step.
- **Items in the economy** (`life2/items-trade.ts`): every working villager owns the tool for their work (`itNpcTool`; `n.toolQ` is its wear and is kept in step);
  a worn-out one is replaced at the smith or the Taberna (`itNpcNewTool`). Your tools wear with each use (`IT_WEAR`) and break. Breaking a rock sometimes gives
  iron ore (`itRockOre`: yours to sell to the smith; a stonecutter's goes to the smithy); the caravan still brings a little iron daily (a safety valve).
  The smith's counter offers each tool, buys ore, and sells lockpicks to friends (`itOffers`, `itExtras`). Seeds (`IT_SEEDS`): barley (staple), turnips (quick),
  beans; each gives its own food and a seed or two back (`itHarvest`); the Taberna sells seed; farmers leave the player's garden and sowings alone (`itFarmersPlot`).
  A lockpick in hand opens a house barred for the night (`itPickLock`: it may snap, somebody may hear; a break-in event). The Sawmill: a Miller (hired when food
  allows, sent to the fields when it is short) fells logs and saws them into planks (2 -> 3); planks are sold there and count as timber on a site or a wall.
- The other verbs in `lf2Verbs` (steal, wreck, shove, give, wake, follow ...) are still registered in the hooks but nothing reaches them yet.
  A new action means a key in `targetAt0` / `lifeAct` plus a line in `itNeedOf` saying what must be in hand.

## Islands and saves (src/ui/saves.ts)
Several islands are kept in the browser (IndexedDB `greenhollow`: `meta` rows for the list, `data` rows with the JSON). The game autosaves every ~30 s, when the tab hides and when you leave build mode, and boots into the last island (`gh.last`) or the Islands panel (button "Islands"). New island = Blank (one settler, a tent, nothing built: `blankStart` in `ai/decision-steps.ts`) or Ready village (the old pre-built start). There are no small/medium sizes. `isleCapture`/`isleRestore` encode the whole state graph (shared refs, Maps, Sets, typed arrays), skipping functions and `SAVE_SKIP` keys (plans are re-decided, `n.rand` is rebuilt). The Islands button (`#newTown`) only opens the list (a second handler once made a random new island and the autosave overwrote yours). Islands can be named (`ISLE.label`, shown in the list and the header; Rename on each row) and are deleted with an in-panel two-step button (no `confirm()`: the artifact sandbox blocks dialogs). Bump `SAVE_VERSION` when state changes shape; old saves can simply be deleted. Tests set `window.GH_NOSAVE`. Villager talk has speech bubbles as before, and the two who talk stand still for as long as the bubbles show (`talkUntil`, set in `drainEvents`), so a bubble never drifts away from its speaker; it is also logged in the Chatter tab (with a speech icon).

## Blank island: huts and visitors (src/life2/visitors.ts)
A sealed free room with a bed prop inside and a door becomes a hut (`visHomes`: `kind: 'house'`, `hut: true`, `r.beds` are the bed props themselves, `owner` on the prop; a bed pushed out is let go and its owner is homeless). `housingTick` then moves homeless people into free beds (it runs when you leave build mode, so you get the first bed at once, and when a visitor arrives; a bump into your own loose bed puts you to bed (`lifeAct`), loose beds do not block walking, `lfBedAt` in `world/construction.ts`, so owners can get into them and sleep there). `visitorTick` (hourly, blank scenario only): when a bed is free, a visitor comes ashore (`visArrive`, with a backstory in `n.back` put together at random from an ordinary moment and an unexplained arrival ("was ironing a shirt, and then there was sand"; `VIS_ORD`, `VIS_ARR`: they are confused, ask whether it is a dream, and accept it anyway), 6 grain of their own); the first arrival a day after the first free bed, then one every 3-5 days; from the third on the island needs food stock (`visFood() >= 8 + 6 * pop`). Singles first; couples once there are three people. Alone, `visitorMutter` has you think aloud about what to build next. A hut gets no kitchen hearth (`lf2PlaceHearth` would drop an oven tile in it that outlived the hut; old saves are cleaned by `clearOrphanOvens`). A privy seat (build item `toilet`) inside a built room makes `r.toilet` (a standing spot beside it, `r.ptoilet`; the room is called Privy if it has no bed) and `lfPrivies` offers it to villagers. You keep the same villager for good: `idleRelease` is off (`IDLE_RELEASE`), `setRole` never gives the steered villager a job, and the frame loop takes the reins back if anything lets go. The island label shows when it was last saved (click it to save now); a new seed makes a new island, never overwrites the current one. Villagers shout what they are missing when you are near (`visNeeds`: bed, privy, food; once per six hours each). Newcomers (`n.newcomer`, two days) shout `Land at last!`, walk up to the player and the others to introduce themselves (`visIntroTick`: a greeting, a welcome, a remark about the island) and keep remarking on the island (`VIS_ISLE`). Unowned beds are drawn grey (blanket index 5), owned ones keep their colour. There are no camp tents (`LF_TENTS = false`; the homeless bed down by the square). Walls and doors can only be broken in build mode. Barred doors at night are off (`LF2.feat.nightlocks = false`). Villagers do not leave the island (decided): the unhappy stay unhappy, and those who do not make it die there.

## The balance sheet and the tutorial (src/life2/needs.ts)
`townBalance()` measures the island in numbers: beds (people + 1 until `TUT_TARGET` = 10), privies (1 per `PRIVY_FOR` = 5), food (each person eats `FOOD_PER_HEAD` = 3.6 portions of grain a day, a plot gives `PLOT_YIELD` = 1.25 a day, 1.8 times that with a working baker, and a quarter of the food is fish and berries, so plots needed = ceil(people x 3.6 x 0.75 / yield)). `islandNeeds()` turns what is out of balance into requests in the order the island should grow: your bed, a second bed (one more than people), a privy, a field of the right size, the first harvest, beds for children, then bakery (3+ people and a harvest), smithy (4), tavern and shop (5), chapel (6), pews. The first request is shown big at the top of the Town tab with the balance table under it (`needsHTML`) and voiced by villagers near you. Hard requests (beds for children, a privy, a field, the harvest) stop new visitors (`tutBlocksArrival`), so the island only grows when it can feed and house itself; the bed requests do not, since a free bed is what brings a visitor. Villagers farm your field plots (`tutFarmers` turns gatherers into farmers when plots need work; the villager you steer is not counted in job caps; no builder or labourer jobs on a blank island). `town.tut.harvests` counts harvests. Not done yet: asking a villager "what do you need?" in conversation, mood falling while a request is ignored.

## Materials, the kit and the carpenter (src/life2/kit.ts, carpenter.ts)
On a blank island building uses a kit (`town.kit`, start `KIT_START`: 40 wall blocks, 3 doors, 2 window blocks, 3 beds, a privy seat, 2 chairs, a table, a shelf; the Field brush is free; the ready village is unlimited). Placing takes one (`kitTake` in `bldPlaceFront`), and everything you take down comes back in full (`kitGive`: hammer on a block, a loose thing, house furniture, a lamp, tent or fire), so nobody gets stuck. The build bar and menu show counts; an empty one says so (`kitOut`). More is made by the carpenter (`KIT_RECIPE`: wood for most things, stone for the hearth things). He comes ashore after the first harvest (`carpArrive`, `town.tut.carpenter`, 'was a ship's carpenter') with a carpentry table (`bench`, in your kit). A room with the bench and a shelf is the workshop (`FN_DEFS`), and the carpenter (`LF2_JOBS.Carpenter`, `carpPlan`) makes what `carpWant()` says the island lacks (beds for everybody, walls to keep 12, doors, a privy seat, the furniture of the next building wanted) as wood is in store, straight into your kit. Wood: everybody helps (the `Woodcutter` agenda, `wcPlan`, fells trees and stacks the wood at the workshop), and you hand wood and stone in by walking into the bench. The requests `carpenter`, `workshop` and `wood` say what is missing.

## On-screen request, signs and layout guides (src/ui/hud-needs.ts, life2/signs.ts, guides.ts)
Only the full-screen canvas is shown to a player, so the request lives on it: a pill at the top left ("● A privy", amber when it blocks visitors) that opens into a card with the request, how to meet it and the balance in a few lines (click it or press **N**; the choice is remembered in `gh.req`). Every room has a sign on the wall beside its door (`signWallOf`, `signMap`, drawn by `drawSigns`; walk into it, not in build mode, to hear `signText`: who lives there and free beds, or the purpose of a bakery/smithy/..., or, for an unfinished room, `signUnfinished`: "Unfinished bakery: oven ✓, counter ✗"). The first hut and the first privy come with a layout to follow (`GUIDE_DEFS`, `guidesUpdate` from the HUD tick, `drawGuides`): amber dashed squares for wall blocks, a blue one for the door, ghost furniture inside; they turn green as you fill them and vanish when the building exists. A guide only guides.

## Function rooms and what the island needs (src/life2/functions.ts, needs.ts)
A room you built takes its purpose from the furniture you placed in it (`fnScan`, hourly and when you leave build mode; nothing is added for you): oven + counter = bakery, forge + anvil = smithy, counter + shelf = shop (a loom inside gives the weaver's spot), counter + table + 2 chairs = tavern (`town.tavern`, chairs are `r.seats`), altar + 2 pews = chapel (`town.church`, pews are `r.pews`, a priest is chosen). The room gets that `kind`, a name and the standing spots the jobs use (`fnSpots`: oven, front, work, counter, bar, altar ...), found beside the furniture; a Baker, Smith or Shopkeeper is hired from whoever is free (`lf2AssignJobs`). Take the furniture out and the room goes back to a plain room (`fnRevert`). Such a room is not a home (`r.fn`). Loose chairs and pews are walked over like beds. `islandNeeds()` works out what is missing (beds, a privy, a field, food, bakery, smithy, shop, tavern, chapel, more pews, beds for children) with who says so, shown at the top of the Town tab (`needsHTML`) and voiced by villagers near you (`visNeeds`). With no larder merchants (a blank island) the baker collects what the households can spare from their pantries (`lf2PantryFetch`: more than three portions a head); bump the bakery's counter with grain in your bag to sell it (`sellgrain`), without grain to buy a loaf; the household's morning bread errand is never given to the villager you steer. Not done yet: couples' beds joining up, a real "ask for a bed" request, the old shopkeepers' jobs after demolition.

## Magic mushrooms (src/life2/shrooms.ts)
About 40 magic mushrooms are scattered when an island is made (`shroomScatter`, `town.shrooms`, mostly at the edge of the trees, the same for the same seed); one more may grow each morning (`shroomGrow`, cap `SH_CAP`; built-over ones vanish). Walking into one (not in build mode) eats it (`eatShroom`): `game.trip` starts for `SH_TRIP` game minutes (a few hours; more mushrooms extend it and deepen it). `psyLevel()` (0..~1.3) rises over 25 minutes and fades over the last 50. While it lasts `psyPost` (called at the end of `render`) bends the finished frame: horizontal strips wave, the colours cycle and oversaturate, the last frame echoes, the view breathes, a coloured rim pulses and sparks orbit you; mushrooms glow in rainbow colours; you think odd thoughts (`SH_THOUGHTS`) and a villager nearby may remark on it (`SH_NPC`). Sprite `magic_shroom` (painters.ts). Old saves get their mushrooms on load.

## Your own heart is yours (src/life/aging-romance.ts, ui/hud-needs.ts)
Nobody courts or marries the villager you steer unless you say so. When the village's romance would pair someone with you, `askCourtship` / `askMarriage` set `town.decision` instead: a card in the middle of the screen (Yes / Not yet / No) and **time stands still until you answer** (`answerDecision`; the frame loop skips the sim while `town.decision` is set). Yes starts a courtship (`startCourtship`) or schedules the wedding; Not yet asks again in 6 days (courting) or 8 (marrying); No ends it, costs some affection and is remembered (`refusedAt`, 20 days). Villagers' own romances are unchanged. Tides are gone: `tideLevel()` stands at its middle level and the tide talk and legends are removed. Every privy seat in a closed room with a free tile beside it is a privy (`r.pseats`, `lfPrivies`); seats outside a room are counted as loose (`looseSeats`) and explained in the request. TrueSight adds the points where a wall crosses the edge of the view (`tsPolygon`), so nothing visible is left in shadow when zoomed in.

## Talk (the new engine, src/talk/; design: docs/talk-engine.md)
All the old chat engines are gone (git tag `last-with-old-talk` keeps them). **The player talks too**: walking into a villager (auto controls: a bump talks) opens the talk box through `openTalk` -> `ptBegin` (talk/player.ts); `bumpTalk` still returns false. Only events with `ev.talk` reach the bubbles (`drainEvents` drops everything else, including the lines the old `say()`/`think` calls would push; `say()` is a no-op).
- `talk/registry.ts` `WORLD.has(kind, id)` / `WORLD.name` / `WORLD.ok(requires)`: what exists (features built, items, people). Lexicon lines declare `requires: ['feature:tavern']`; a line whose thing does not exist is never chosen.
- `talk/memory.ts` `TM`: `n.tmem` (bounded, scored by importance and age): `talked` memories now; news, versions and reflection are phase 2.
- `talk/acts.ts` `ACTS`: acts are data (`kind` = switch group in `TALK.on`, `stage`, `when`, `w` weight, `apply`, `replies`). Phase 1: greet, greet_back, remark_time/place/work/state, ask_how/answer_how, agree, pre_close, farewell, farewell_back. Add an act by adding an entry; never edit the engine.
- `talk/lexicon.ts` `LEX` / `lx(act, pack, text, tags, requires)` / `lxSay(act, sp, li, c)`: the words. Tags are conditions (band, rel, state, trait); more matching tags = more specific = preferred; lines the listener heard lately are penalised; `TALK_PACK.cur` picks the voice, `plain` is the fallback. The Murakami `island` pack is phase 4.
- `talk/shape.ts` `talkRun(a, b, listened)`: open (greet) -> topics (speaker raises one, listener answers, turn goes to whoever has more patience left, `talkBudget`) -> pre_close -> farewell. Logs acts in `TALK.log` (`lines` only when somebody could hear, or `TALK.words = true` for tests) and pushes one event with `talk: true`. Both remember it (`TM`), social need and affection rise a little.
- `talk/schedule.ts` `talkTick` (an `LF2.tickers` entry): an idle villager (standing/at leisure, nobody called away from work) with company within `TALK.range` (10) walks up (`talkApproach`: 'Going over to X', then stands beside them), talks, and the interrupted plan resumes. Words are only looked up when the player is within `TALK.earshot` (16). Switches: `TALK.on[kind]`, `TALK.running`.
- **Phase 2 (news and memory)**: `lfEmit` (life2/core.ts) is the world-event bus; it calls `TE.onEmit` (`talk/events.ts`) which gives the actor a `did`, the target a `felt` and each witness a `saw` memory in `n.tmem` (`{ kind, ev, evk, conf, imp, from, hop, ver: { who, sev, note }, told }`). Conversation moves them: `share_news` (speaker picks the most important recent thing the listener does not know and that is not about either of them, `TE.pick`) gives the listener a `told` version, less sure each hop (`TE.tell`), now and then changed (bigger, softer, harder, nameless, shifted to someone the teller dislikes); the listener reacts by appraisal (`react_shock/amused/sympathy/doubt/delight/unmoved`, `TE.appraise`, valences from `LF2_TAGS`), opinion of the one in the story moves (`lfAff`), and people within 6 tiles overhear at lower confidence (`TE.overhear`). `ask_news` is for the one with nothing to tell. The card shows "Knows" with where each thing came from and how sure. Event wording: `lx('ev:<kind>', 'plain', '{a} ... {t}')` templates; a kind without one falls back to the old sentence in `ev.text` (to be replaced as the emitters are rewritten). `visArrive` emits an `arrival` event. Every turn keeps the context its words need (`turn.x`), because words are looked up after the whole conversation.
- **Seeing who talks to whom**: speech bubbles carry `to` (the listener). `bubbleBehind` puts every speaker's bubble BEHIND them, opposite to the way they face (`n.face`; they face the one they talk to, so two people talking left to right have their bubbles at the outer sides and never overlap): facing south = bubble above with the tail below; north = below; east = left, tail on its right; west = right, tail on its left, always pointing at the speaker (`bubbleSide`, the side that faces the viewer, is only the fallback for somebody with no facing); a ring under the speaker and a dotted thread to the listener say who talks to whom. People stand on the whole tile orthogonally next to each other (`talkSpot`, never a diagonal, same room) facing each other, and the talk only starts when they are exactly 1 tile apart. Words are looked up for whatever you can see or hear (on screen or within `TALK.earshot`). The wait step while they gather is `nopie`. **Needs pause while talking** (`talkPaused`: hunger, starving, bladder, energy, fun, social, health and joy do not run while `talkUntil` is in the future); nobody starts a chat with an urgent need (hunger over 92, bladder over 85). In the evening (17-22h) people on a walk who are not hungry stop for a word (`TALK.stroll`, `talkLeisure`; by day it starved the island); the one stopped and the one who walks over resume their plans afterwards. Conversations are short (budget 2.2, about 8-12 turns). **H = hyper talk** (developer mode, badge at top): `TALK.hyper` gives constant short talks near the player, ignores what people are doing, quicker bubbles; it costs the economy, for testing only.
### The contract (talk/registry.ts, kinds.ts, life.ts; rules and checks in docs/talk-engine.md 3c-3d)
- **Game code never writes words and talk code never reads game internals.** The game reports what happens with `LIFE.emit(kind, who, data)` (structured data); only the lexicon has words. Every event is remembered by `who` (`n.tmem`, `n.day` log), counted (`town.lifeStats`, `LIFE.digest(days)`), and kept in `LIFE.stream`.
- **`REG`** (talk/registry.ts) holds what things *are* (`REG.define(kind, id, meta)`, kinds `event`, later `item`, `crop`, `place`, `feature`, `job`); `WORLD` says what *exists now*. **`talk/kinds.ts` is the one list of event kinds** (`EVK(id, family, { needs, tell, tags })`): add a kind there, one line. Unregistered kinds or missing `needs` throw in tests (`GH_LIFE_STRICT`, set by tests/lib.mjs) and warn once otherwise.
- **Adding something to the game**: define its kinds/ids in `kinds.ts`/`REG`, emit with `LIFE.emit` where the thing happens (or, until a system is converted, report it from `lifeStep` in life.ts, the only adapter that reads plan steps: sleeping, privy and eating are reported there), add lexicon lines (`lx('ev:<kind>', ...)`). Removing something: remove its definition; the lint test flags lexicon lines that still name it.
- `lfEmit` (life2/core.ts) is an adapter into the same stream (`LIFE.fromLegacy`) and still feeds `TE.onEmit` (news memories). The 38 old kinds are registered.
- Checks that run in `npm test`: refusal of unregistered kinds/missing data, counting and day log, legacy events through the stream, every lexicon line names a defined act or a registered kind, deleting a definition flags its lines, a normal day reports sleeping and eating.
- **Life slice (the ordinary day)**: real emitters: `bought`/`sold` in `logTrade` (economy/goods.ts), `harvested` in `itHarvest`, `planted` where a farmer sows (ai/work-plans.ts), and, through the adapters in life.ts, `slept`/`slept_rough`/`used_privy`/`no_privy`/`bladder_emergency`/`ate` (`lifeStep`, when a plan step begins) and `went_hungry`/`starving`/`tired` (`lifeWatch`, hourly). `LIFE.recent(n, listener)` groups somebody's last 18 hours by kind and thing (six plantings are one line), scores by tellability (troubles count more) and drops what is not worth saying (score under 0.16) and kinds with no `life:<kind>` line. Acts (switch kind `life`): `ask_day` -> `report_day` / `nothing_much`, answered by `empathise` (troubles) or `acknowledge`; the listener keeps a `told_life` memory of how the other one is. Lines are first person: `lx('life:<kind>', 'plain', 'I planted {things} today.')`, with `{things}` = count + name said the way people say it (`lxCount`), names from `REG` (goods and seeds are defined from `GOODS` and `IT_SEEDS`, so a new good or seed is talkable at once). A kind with no line is never told, so add the line when you add the kind. `LIFE.troubles(days)` counts troubles (`LIFE_TROUBLE` in kinds.ts) by kind and by distinct person; the request card shows "This week: 6 people slept rough · 4 people could not find a privy".
- **Shop talk (talk/service.ts, kind `trade`)**: every purchase or sale a villager makes at a stall is a short exchange in bubbles between the customer and the keeper (`talkService(cust, keeper, { mode: 'buy' | 'sell', item, qty, price, result: 'ok' | 'none' | 'poor' | 'refused', shop, newItem })`, called from `doBuy`/`doSell` and `farmSeedBuy`): greet - greet back - ask for - quote - pay - thank (- 'new in this week' - farewell); out of stock or too poor and refused sales have their own endings (`serviceScript`). The player's stall menu (`farmShopDialog`) speaks the keeper's lines from the same lexicon (`serviceSay`), so a stall talks the same to everyone. `lxMoney` says "one denarius", "6 denarii". **Prices in conversation**: `ask_price` -> `tell_price` -> `react_price`: a villager asks about an item they have no price for and the other, who paid or got one lately (`LIFE.lastPrice`, from their day log), says what it was; the asker keeps a `price` memory. `talkFinish(c, a, b, listened)` (shape.ts) is the shared tail of every conversation (words, log, bubbles, holds) and `talkListened` decides when words are looked up.
- **Player talk (talk/player.ts)**: the same engine with a different chooser. `ptBegin(o, p)` greets (player `greet`, villager `greet_back`) and shows a menu; `ptOptions` lists up to five things the player could say now (acts whose `when` holds for the player, said in the player's voice by the lexicon, always one small-talk remark, never more than five) and **when the villager has just asked something the options are the answers that fit** (`S.pending`); `ptStep(key)` plays the chosen act (`talkTurn`), the villager answers by their own chooser (`talkChoose` over the act's `replies`) and, if they have patience left, brings something up (always says something back); when their patience is gone they pre-close and the only option is goodbye. Acts that carry state in `c` (`report_day` -> `c.life`, `share_news` -> `c.news`, `ask_price` -> `c.priceItem`) have a `preview(c, sp, li)` that says what the option's words need, and the chosen option restores that state before it is applied. `ptEnd()` (from `closeTalk`) makes both remember it (`talked`), moves opinion and acquaintance a little, and logs the acts (`TALK.log` entry with `player: true`). The player has a day log too (`LIFE` events for planting, harvest, sales), so `report_day` works for you. In the farming world, bumping a stall keeper opens the stall menu (`farmShopDialog`) instead. `talkTurn`, `talkWords`, `talkX` (shape.ts) are the pieces both choosers share. The test `player talk` plays it through the real frame loop with real key presses and clicks.
- **Barks (talk/barks.ts)**: stall keepers (the merchants and the baker at the counter) call out to people passing within 8 tiles every 25-65 minutes (`keeperCalls`, an `LF2.tickers` entry): `keeper_call` from the stall's real stock and prices (`keeperCallFor`: what is new this week, what is cheap, what is plain), and the passer-by sometimes answers (`call_reply`: "Maybe later."). Lines: `lx('keeper_call', 'plain', 'New in this week: {item}.', { call: 'new' })` (tags `call` = 'new' | 'cheap' | 'plain'). Said in bubbles toward the passer-by like any talk (`talkFinish`).
- **Bindings (talk/shape.ts, read this before writing an act)**: an act's `when(c, sp, li)` may write what the act would be about on the conversation (`c.about`, `c.news`, `c.whereObj` ...). It is always run through `talkBind`, against a *view* of the conversation (`Object.create(c)`), so those writes stay with the candidate and the real conversation is untouched; the chooser (`talkChoose`) keeps the binding of its pick in `c.bound` and `talkTurn(c, sp, li, id, bind?)` puts it on the conversation just before `apply`. The player's menu (`ptOptions`) keeps each option's binding (plus whatever its `preview` settled, e.g. whom to blame) and `ptStep` passes it to `talkTurn`, so an option says what its label said. Never call `A.when(c, ...)` on a live conversation outside `talkBind`. **Slots**: `lxSay` builds one slot table (`lxSlots`) and only says a line whose slots all have values (`about`, `item`, `things`, `qty`, `price`, `news`, `deed`, `them` have no default; `reason`, `shop`, `who`, `money`, `state` do); a line that cannot be said is counted in `LX_MISS` (the bindings test and the playtest harness fail when it is not empty), and a menu option with no sayable line is not offered. `docs/talk-review.md` is the review of the engine with the task list (numbered tasks for a smaller model, lettered ones needing design).
- **Agenda and thread (talk/shape.ts)**: a conversation serves what each came to say. `talkRitual(a, b)` picks the ceremony (0 none: family at home or people who spoke within the hour; 1 a greeting and one farewell; 2 the full open and close); `talkAgenda(c, sp, li)` is each one's top two purposeful acts (`TALK_PURPOSE`: news, a grievance, a favour, the day, romance ...) and they are raised first, small talk is capped (`smallMax`) and a talk with nothing to say does not happen. **Shared ground**: `talkNote` records what one has said to the other (`sp.saidTo[li.id]`, by act and subject) and `talkStale` keeps it from being said again for `TALK_AGAIN[kind]` hours. **Thread**: `talkAnswers` chains replies (four deep) and an act may name follow-ups (`A.follow`, e.g. `ask_family` -> `ask_why_state` -> `explain_state`, `ask_day` -> `ask_more_day`), so an answer can be asked about. `ask_news` can be answered with one's own day (`report_day`).
- **Small life (life2/smalllife.ts, `sys.smalllife`)**: things worth saying come from ordinary days. Hourly (`smallLifeHour`): people of one job working within 7 tiles (`worked_with`), a meal at the same table (`ate_with`, not kin), a hand lent to somebody at work (`lent_hand`, news), a visit to the ill (`sick_visit`), a find out of doors (`found`, `SMALL_FINDS`, `n.trinket`), illness for a day or two (`n.ill`, `ill`/`recovered`, state `ill`). Each writes a reason in the opinion ledger (`lfAff`: 'worked beside me', 'ate with me', 'lent me a hand'), so opinions have real reasons, and is a life event with first-person lines. `snap` -> `snap_back`/`let_it_go`: somebody hungry, tired or ill is short with the other.
- **The voice rule (talk/lexicon.ts, pack-short.ts)**: a line with an aside (a second sentence, or a long one: `lxMarked`) is allowed at most one time in four per speaker (`sp.sinceAside`) and never on a menu label; the other lines are short and plain. `talk/pack-short.ts` holds the plain island lines for the most-used acts (keep it plain; put asides elsewhere). No tic or run-on is added on top of an aside. `node tools/lexpool.mjs <acts...>` counts short and marked lines per act, `tools/lextags.mjs` lists lines with tags, `tools/actlist.mjs` lists topics and replies.
- **The player's menu says what an option does (talk/player.ts)**: `PT_LABEL[act] = [group, label]` ("Ask after {about}", "Tell the news: {news}", "Blame {about}"); options are ordered by group (`PT_GROUPS`: Answer, Ask, Tell, Give, Heart, Small talk); the words are chosen when it is picked and shown as what you said (`r.you` -> `talkUI.q`). An act with no entry shows its line. A new act needs a `PT_LABEL` entry.
- **Voice and people (talk/pack-island.ts, people.ts, ui/hud-journal.ts)**: `town.pack` ('plain' for the ready village, 'island' for blank and farm; J panel has a Voice select) picks the lexicon pack; the island pack is dry, exact, no exclamation marks. `lxTic` gives some people a verbal habit (statements only) and `LX_RECENT` stops a line anyone said in the last 3 hours being picked again. **People acts** (switch kind `person`): `ask_opinion` -> `give_opinion`/`no_view`, `gossip_person` -> `agree_opinion`/`differ_opinion`, `ask_family` -> `answer_family`; the opinion comes with a reason from the real ledger `sbWhy(a, b, v, why)` (`a.why[b.id]`), and the listener keeps an `opinion` memory and shifts their own view a little. **Journal (L)**: the steered villager's `tmem` as a card: news (saw/told/overheard, how sure), what people say about each other, prices, how people are.
- **Meddling (life2/meddle.ts, talk/deeds.ts)**: build mode stops time, so a deed is *noted* the moment it is done (`meddleNote(kind, x, y, by, { room, residents, place })`: who was near, asleep, on the privy, who could see you via `tsLine`) and *settled* when time runs again (`meddleFlush`, from `updateSim`): one `lfEmit` event (`wall_removed`, `home_destroyed`, `building_down`, plus `caught_on_privy` for anyone on a privy step in the room; sleepers carried out of doors give `bed_moved` from the ticker in push.ts) with witnesses, **anonymous if nobody awake saw you** (the memories of those who only know it happened carry no name, `TE.onEmit`). Hooked in `bldBreak` and `bldDemolish` (sealing.ts). Those who saw you cry out (`cry_deed`, one-line bubble) and think worse of you (`lfAff`, with a reason), the people it happened to lose mood. Then it is news (`ev:` lines in deeds.ts) and **they bring it up with you**: `raise_deed` (kind `deed`, switch `TALK.on.deed`) when the villager holds a deed of yours (`deedPick`: saw/felt/told, tags harm/nuisance/embarrass/scandal, within 8 days); in the player's talk menu a villager with a grievance opens with it (`ptBegin`) and your answers are `deed_sorry/defend/deny/shrug`, theirs `deed_forgive/cold/believe/doubt`, each moving opinion. The journal (L) has "What you did" with how many know it was you. More deeds: `furniture_removed` (a bed or chair of a home or an owned loose bed, hammer), `person_pushed` (an awake villager pushed about), and exposures found in the room or near it (`caught_on_privy`, `caught_bathing`, `caught_praying`, by plan step text), each with its own cry (`cry_deed` how `furniture`/`pushed`/`bath`/`pray`) and chronicle names for the big ones ("The Day of the Open Privy"). **Reactions** are plans (`meddleReactions` sets `n.react`, the `LF2.dec` entry in meddle.ts makes the plan): stare at the damage, flee mortified, tell a friend (`meddleTell`: the friend hears it at once), or go and wait beside you to have it out. Not yet: villagers rebuilding, the watch acting on it.
- **Gifts (talk/gifts.ts, kind `gift`)**: `offer_gift` is the player's act (also offered as an answer when somebody raises a deed): the best thing in your goods for them (`giftChoose`: food when they are hungry, flowers always) moves to their goods, warms them (`lfAff`, with the reason 'gave me X'), emits `gift` (news), and they answer `gift_thanks/delight/amends/refuse`. A gift after a deed is an amends: the deed memories are marked `forgiven` and are not raised again (`deedPick`). Somebody below -45 refuses and you keep it. **Everybody gives** (rule for everything in the talk engine: the same acts for the player and every villager, so it is easy to test): villagers give what they can spare (never the last three, no food when hungry themselves, once in twelve hours), more to the hungry, the liked, and those they wronged (`w`), and it is made up for (`forgiven`).
- **Requests, favours and feelings (talk/requests.ts)**: `reqWant(n)` is what somebody really lacks (food when hungry with none, a bed when homeless). `ask_favour` (kind `economy`, same for everyone; the player's talk menu opens with it when a villager has one for you, like a grievance) -> the other answers `favour_give` (goods change hands; closes the request, `help` event), `favour_promise` (player only: an open thread in your `tmem`, listed under "Open threads" in the journal) or `favour_no`; `chase_favour` when they remind you of a promise. Threads close when it happens or the bed is found (`LF2.hourly`), and a promise left three days fails with a grudge. Feelings (kind `feeling`): `vent` (hungry/tired/low, to a friend) -> `console`/`shrug_off`, `praise` -> `thank_praise`; talking about it lifts mood a little. Law (accuse/confess/question) and groups/interruption are parked on purpose.
- **Secrets (talk/secrets.ts, kind `secret`)**: `confide` (everyone: a close friend tells a deed of theirs, or something that shamed them, that few know; the player confesses their anonymous deeds the same way): the listener gets a `told` memory marked `secret` with its `keeper`, and answers `secret_keep` or, for serious ones, `secret_alarmed`. A secret is not gossip (`TE.pick` skips it unless the teller is gossipy, 12%, or is the player, who may choose to). Passing one on (`TE.tell` -> `secretLeak`) queues a leak; a day later the keeper learns of it (`LF2.hourly`), thinks the worse of the teller and a `betrayal` event is raised with them like any deed (`deedYou`). The journal has "Secrets you keep". `TM.add` returns the stored record, so flags set on it stick.
- **Romance (talk/romance.ts, kind `romance`)**: `flirt` -> `flirt_back`/`flirt_rebuff`, `declare` -> `declare_return`/`declare_gentle`, `ask_to_walk` -> `walk_yes`/`walk_later`/`walk_no` (yes = `startCourtship`), `propose` -> `propose_yes`/`propose_later`/`propose_no` (yes = `scheduleWedding`). Same for everyone, same pairing rules (`romOK`), needs the `sys.romance` / `event.wedding` modules; the village's own daily romance still runs; the player answers in the menu.
- **Reputation, suspicion and blame (talk/reputation.ts)**: `repTags(obs, subj)` is what one person believes subj did (from their event memories, by who they *believe* did it), `repVillage(subj)` the village's word, `repWord` the one word that colours a greeting (`rep` tag in `lxContext`, `greet_back` lines) and `tell_rep` tells a friend what is said. An unseen deed is guessed at by each person who learns of it (`repSuspect`: grudge, stranger, past deeds, the real doer now and then, or no idea; memory `ver.suspect`, confidence at most 0.75), so an innocent can be blamed and the blame travels as news. The accuser raises it (`raise_deed`, how `suspect`); the innocent can only deny/shrug (`deed_deny` is believed and clears it, `cleared`); the guilty can `deed_blame` somebody else or own up (`confide` puts a wrong version right). The journal shows "What folks say about you" and who is blamed for your deeds.
- **Tastes, sent gifts, apologies, jealousy (talk/gifts.ts, amends.ts)**: everybody likes two goods and dislikes one, fixed by who they are (`giftTastes`); others find out by asking (`ask_preference` -> `tell_preference`), by a hint (`hint_gift`), by a gift going down well or badly (`gift_loved`/`gift_disliked`), or because they are family; what a person knows is `pref` memories (`giftKnown`). `giftChoose` prefers a known like and never a known dislike. A gift is owed back (`n.owes`, `offer_gift` how `return`). `send_gift` asks someone to take a gift to a third person without saying who (`n.parcel`, a plan in `LF2.dec`, `parcelGive`): an anonymous `gift` event, the receiver guesses the sender (`repSuspect`) and may credit the wrong person. `apologise` -> `accept_apology`/`reject_apology` is sorry said before anyone asks (marks `forgiven`); `confront_jealous` (a crush or partner who hears of a courting with someone else) -> `jealous_explain`/`jealous_defiant` (may end a courtship)/`jealous_deny`.
- **Conversation depth (talk/depth.ts, reputation.ts)**: topics after the first are *linked* (`Speaking of which`) or a *fresh start* (`Anyway.`) by subject (`talkLinkOf`, `lxLink`, not in the player's menu), conversations chain replies up to four deep (`talkRun`), a **story** is `story_preface` -> `story_invite` -> `share_news` (`c.storyNews`), `callback` remembers the last subject (`talked` memory `subj`), `reflect` (3am) turns patterns in `n.why` into `n.refl` opinions, and the small acts `joke`/`laugh`/`groan`, `wonder`, `ask_work`/`answer_work`, `ask_where`/`tell_where`, `warn`, `ask_advice`/`give_advice`, `brag_child`, `tell_back` (the backstory) exist for everyone. Barks: `mutter` at work, a greeting between people passing, `call_child` at dusk (`moreBarks`). News told in your hearing goes in your journal as overheard (`talkFinish`); the journal also shows tastes you know, jobs, and the path a rumour came by; the villager card has a Notice row (what they lack, what they think of you, what they hide). `TALK.dial` (J panel) sets how much stories change and how hard deeds land; `node tools/talkstats.mjs [days] [scenario]` prints how the talk is doing. Family lines: `rel` is `spouse`/`partner`/`child`/`parent`/`sibling`/`kin` (a `kin` line fits any relative). Sound: `sndMurmur` for talk you cannot see.
- **Markets**: the seed stall (`stall_seed_*`) and the greengrocer (`stall_veg_*`) have their own art, chosen by the keeper's shop (`stallKindAt`), and each stall shows an OPEN/SHUT board (`drawStallSigns`, life2/market-signs.ts). There is no rest day any more (`REST_DAY = -1`). NPC sight cones are off by default (`SIGHT_VIS.on`, V toggles). Speech bubbles read `NAME: text` and sit behind the speaker. Pulling down a house leaves each owner their own bed as a loose bed (`keepBed` in `bldDemolish`).
- **Playtest harness (tools/playtest.mjs)**: `node tools/playtest.mjs <scenario> [--days=6] [--seed=41] [--out=dir]` plays the game without you: a bot steers a villager (talks to the nearest people through the real talk menu choosing answers by persona, and in the `meddle` scenario knocks walls out, takes a bed, shoves somebody, hides a deed) while the game runs for days of game time, then writes `<out>/<scenario>.md` (verdict, numbers, things said too often, text slips, what the village thinks of the bot, who knew and who was blamed for each deed, daily snapshots, the bot's own talks, everything every villager said) and `.json`. Scenarios: `village` (a quiet week on the farming island), `meddle`, `social` (talk to everybody all day), `romance` (three couples in the making). It lints every line and every menu option (placeholders, stray punctuation, a/an, doubled words, exclamation marks in the island voice, empty options), counts which acts never fire and which lines repeat. Read the `.md` as a player would; the lint only finds the mechanical slips. Menu labels need every slot filled: an act whose answer is shown to the player needs a `preview` that supplies what its words need.
- Not yet: reactions to furniture removal, the watch and meddling, favours (asking for things), romance gifts. (Old list:) meddling events and reactions (3), the island voice and tics (4), people/opinions (5), feelings and favours (6), gifts (7), romance (8), law and secrets (9), barks and groups (10). News has one path: conversation (`TE.tell`, `TE.overhear`); `TM.addEv` mirrors what is told into the old ledger `n.kn`, which the watch and the law (life2/law.ts) still read, and the old word-of-mouth ticker is deleted. `lfEmit` and `lf2Learn` in `life2/core.ts` remain (first-hand knowledge, the watch) and are to be folded into `LIFE.emit` and the memory engine; `talk/base.ts` keeps quiet stand-ins for the old calls (`cvSpeak`, `sbTrust`, ...): delete one when its call site is rewritten.
Voice rules for island text (phase 4): ordinary people calmly accepting an impossible situation, dry, specific, understated, no exclamation marks, exact numbers.

  readability and a week of freshness, items in the economy (villager tools, wear and the smith, iron, seeds and the garden, lockpicks, the sawmill), the dialogue lint, every follow-up answered and ask-about, (85 tests: the old dialogue tests are gone, five talk-engine tests added).
- The random stream is shared, so any change shifts every later random draw: a test that suddenly fails in one town is often exposing a real

- When behaviour changes on purpose, tests may need new expectations; when it changes by accident, they catch it.

## What the player can see: TrueSight (src/render/truesight.ts)
Visibility, not lighting (night, lamps and shadows stay in render/lighting-wild.ts). Buildings are polygons whose walls are as thick as they are drawn:
every wall tile is a solid square and the segments are the straight edges of the solid parts, merged along each wall. So a building's outer corner casts
from the outermost point of its corner tile, a doorway is a real opening between two jambs (the beam through it starts at the inside corners of the door
frame), and a window is a gap in the middle of its tile with a solid piece either side.
From the player's eye a ray goes to every segment end (and a hair either side), stopping at the first segment; the hit points in angle order are the
visibility polygon. Everything outside it is covered in near-black, cut along those straight lines. Only what a sight line reaches is shown: the wall
behind each visible stretch of face (`tsFaces`: a strip one tile deep), so a glancing view shows a sliver; no tile is ever lit as a whole (doors included).
- ONLY buildings block sight: walls, closed doors, the solid bits beside a window. Trees, rocks, furniture, crates and people never do.
- At an inner corner (the face ends at the segment's own end and solid wall carries on round it) `tsFaces` shows the corner tile too, so the four outer corner tiles of a room are not black notches when you stand inside.
- Only one plate of a hole casts at a time: from above (eye north of the tile) the light is cast from the bottom plate, from below from the top plate (`pf`/`pty` flags in `tsGeo`, honoured by `tsRay`). `tsLab` has a wall of test holes (`town.tsHoles`, kinds in `TS_HOLES`: slit, narrow, wide, low, high, archway, window) to compare shapes.
- Windows have their upright dividers (`bars`) as thin solids in both plates, so each pane casts its own beam and the divider casts a dark line; the horizontal divider lies along the line of sight and casts nothing from above.
- Side windows: a window block in a wall that runs up and down (floor to its east or west) becomes a window in a side wall (`town.winS`, drawn as the `window_side` overlay on the wall top); in TrueSight its plates are upright (`tsHoleRot`, `TS_HOLES.windowSide`). The closed sides of a hole tile are blockers only (`pf` 3) and never light a face.
- A window is a hole in a wall tile (`TS_HOLES`, fractions of the tile, taken from the glass of the sprite): two thin plates, solid but for the hole, at its top and bottom edges, so sight passes only through the hole and the lit strip of a window plate stops at its own tile. A new hole shape is a new entry.
- A window tile shows its whole face wherever its outer line can be seen (`tsFaces`), so the wall beside it never shades the tile; only the glass (the `TS_HOLES` rectangle) follows the sight line through it, lit where you can see through and dark where you cannot.
- `tsGeo()` builds (and caches, keyed by `town.gridV` and door states) the segments in a cell grid; `tsRay` finds the nearest hit; `tsPolygon`, `tsWalls`.
  `tsSees(x, y)` = can the player see a person standing there (`canSee` uses it; hidden people are not drawn, picked or named).
  `tsLine(ax, ay, bx, by)` = a clear line on the same segments, for NPC perception with its own rules (NPCs still use the older `seeThrough`).
- Public buildings keep their doors open by day (`tsPropDoors`). Zoomed out to the map (zoom < 0.6) nothing is hidden.
- `node tools/truesight.mjs [--out=dir]` stamps a test ground (`tsLab`: rooms with an archway and a shut door, a corridor with three corners, a house
  with windows, trees, a shed, walls touching corner to corner), checks who is seen from each spot (and while people walk), and saves screenshots. Tests run it.
- What you cannot see is `TS_SHADE` (70%) black in the place you are in and `TS_DARK` (90%) black elsewhere and inside buildings (`TS_SHADE`) in the place you are in, and fully dark elsewhere: out of doors the open ground is half dark and buildings stay black; inside a room (`roomAt`) that room and its walls are half dark and the whole outside goes really dark.
- **Villager sight** (`drawSightCones`, `sightPoly`, V toggles, `SIGHT_VIS`): each villager sees a cone (`SIGHT_RANGE` 7 tiles, `SIGHT_HALF` either side of `n.face`) cut by the same building geometry, drawn light red on the ground. A debugging view for stealth; not yet used by NPC perception.
- ~0.2 ms per recompute at normal zoom, ~0.5 ms zoomed out, only when the eye, the view or a door changes.

## Levels: stairs, roofs and the earth below (src/life2/stairs.ts, growLevels in world/construction.ts)
The map is stacked downwards in blocks of `town.h0` rows (`LV_N` = 5, `lvlOf(y)`): block 0 the ground, 1 and 2 the levels above it (roof decks), 3 and 4 the earth below (mines). It is ordinary map, so building, sealing, props, rooms, walking, TrueSight and NPC sight work on every level unchanged, only the coordinates differ (`growLevels`: saves made before get the levels on load; ground things pick places among `GH()` rows only, so the random stream is unchanged; `town.ore` is 0 none / 1 iron / 2 copper / 3 gold, laid in veins, richer in the second mine). Tiles: `T.SKY` (nothing, transparent), `T.DECK`, `T.STAIR`/`T.STAIRD` (up, and the way down from the deck), `T.EARTH` (solid, dig it), `T.DIRTF` (dug-out floor), `T.STAIRM` (a stair down, on the ground) / `T.STAIRU` (its way up, in the mine). `bldBare(y)` is what a removed wall leaves (grass, deck or dirt).
- **Stairs up** (build item `stair`, kit 1, 6 wood): in build mode stand on a floor tile of a room facing a clear floor tile; the stair goes in front of you and you stand on its landing. A deck is laid over the whole footprint of that building one level up (`stairFootprint`; neighbouring decks join) and you can build on it; a room up there takes stairs to the third level. Stairs up are refused on a bare roof (they need a room's floor). Doors apply only on the ground level: upstairs (`isUpperY`) a closed ring of walls is a room with no door and no way out needed (`bldExit` is true, `bldHasDoor` skipped); stairs down in a mine are drawn (`T.STAIRM`/`T.STAIRU`). You cannot place a stair while standing on another staircase's landing: step away first.
- **Stairs down** (`stairdown`): on open ground (or deep in a mine) a stair goes into the earth: a 3 x 3 chamber of dug-out floor, surrounded by solid earth. Bump the stair to use it. Digging: the hammer on `T.EARTH` (`digAction`, key `dig..`, needs the hammer) clears it; ore gives iron, copper or gold (`n.inv`), now and then stone; the map's edge and the bottom rows are solid. Underground you can build rooms (sealing accepts `T.DIRTF`) or just dig out houses.
- **One way in, mirrored**: a stair is used only by walking into it from its landing (`stairAction`; from anywhere else it just says so). Stairs UP stand just north of their landing (you climb walking north); stairs DOWN just south of theirs (you descend walking south). In build mode `stairPlace` puts a stair up on the floor north of where you stand and a stair down south of you. The stair at the other end is in the same place on its floor with its own landing (north of the deck's down stair, south of the mine's up stair), one block from it. The artwork is the up stair turned over for the down stair, arrows included (`stair_up`, `stair_down`, `stairArrow24`). Using it is a straight cut (`teleportTo`); you can go straight back with a fresh press toward the stair you came out of (a key still held from before does not, see below). Stairs also work in build mode (not with the hammer out). A landing serves one staircase only. You arrive ON the landing (a free tile beside it when somebody is standing there, `stairArrivalTile`) and a key still held from before does not walk you off it (`p.stairHold`, honoured by the keydown handler). **Pushing through stairs**: anything you can push (people, loose things, rocks, furniture, beds with sleepers) standing on a landing, pushed into its stair, comes out on the landing at the other end (`stairPushTarget`, used by `lf2PushLine`; furniture arrives as a loose thing, `pushFurnitureStairs`), up or down; in a line the front piece goes through and the rest follow onto the landing, and the pusher can climb after them. A thing left standing on a landing blocks it.
- `town.portals` maps each landing to its partner, `town.stairs` lists the pairs `{ s0, l0, s1, l1, kind }`; `findPath` and `refreshReach` follow the portals (cost 3), `stepNpc` teleports at a non-adjacent step (`teleportTo`), so villagers use stairs like anybody. Up on a roof the level below is drawn dimmed underneath (`render`), and a deck hides what is under it; mines are drawn as dirt with ore flecks, lit only by what TrueSight shows (earth is solid to sight).
- **Huts on other levels**: a hut (sealed room with a bed and a door) upstairs or underground is a home like any other (`housingTick` looks at every free bed; a flood that reaches a staircase landing counts as a way out in `bldFlood`), and villagers walk there by the stairs. `levelWord(y)` / `placeOf(n)` say where somebody is ("upstairs", "the roof", "the mine"), shown in `npcDebug` (build mode hover).
- **Torches** (build item `torch`, kit 8, 1 wood makes 3): a loose prop (`kind: 'torch'`, art `torch`). Underground the day and night lighting is replaced by darkness (`drawMineDark` in render/truesight.ts) with a small glow round you (`MINE.own`) and a wider warm one round every torch (`MINE.torch`); TrueSight still decides what a sight line reaches.
- Not done yet: taking down the building below should take its deck with it, hammering a stair, villagers' beds and jobs upstairs and underground, ore being used (the smith buys iron ore as before; copper and gold only sell), mine lighting and air, cave-ins.

## Sound (src/ui/sound.ts)
All sound is synthesised in the browser with the Web Audio API (no files): `sndTone` and `sndNoise` build the short sounds (steps by surface `sndStep`, `sndDig`, `sndOre`, `sndStairs`, `sndPlace`, `sndPush`, `sndWork` for trees and rocks, `sndPick` for a harvest, `sndMagic` for a mushroom), and `sndAmbient` (called from `render`) keeps a quiet bed going: wind (stronger out of doors), birds by day, crickets at night, waves near water, and underground a low drone, drips and torch crackle. What happens away from you is quieter with distance and silent from another level (`sndAt`). The audio starts on the first key or click and **M** mutes it (`gh.mute`). The tests set `window.GH_NOSOUND`, so no audio context is ever made under test.

## Work upstairs and underground, and what farmers sow
- **Miners** (`minePlan`, `mineWork`, `mineSpots` in life2/stairs.ts; agenda `Miner` in `pickAgenda`, only when the island is fed, one per eight people): a villager goes down the stairs, stands beside solid earth next to the dug-out floor (ore first, then the nearest to the stairs) and digs it; iron goes to the smithy (the smith's stock) and pays a little, copper and gold are sold. `MINE_CAP` (900 tiles) stops it growing for ever.
- **Roof gardens**: the field brush works on a deck, so a roof can be a garden (`bldBrushStep`); farmers work any plot they can walk to, on any level; digging a plot back leaves `bldBare(y)`.
- **Farmers sow a mix** (`farmSeed` in ai/work-plans.ts): half barley (the staple), the rest the other food crops, each villager with a favourite or two; flowers are left to you. Plots remember their seed (`c.seed`) and grow and give their own food (`itGrow`, `itHarvest`).

## Work clothes (src/render/main.ts `JOB_DRESS`, `jobDress`, `gaSpec`; hats in art/painters.ts `hatL`)
Adults dress for the job they do now (`n.job`, else the agenda `n.cur`, else the role; anybody with a stall `n.shop` wears an apron): farmers a wide straw hat, miners a hard hat with a lamp (`hardhat`), bakers and cooks a chef's toque and apron, smiths and carpenters a cap and apron, millers a headscarf and apron, weavers a bonnet and apron, fishers a floppy hat, woodcutters a beanie, shopkeepers, merchants, barkeeps and bath keepers an apron. It is worked out each frame in `gaSpec` (so a villager who changes work changes clothes), always worn (`hatOn`), and the bath and the watch's uniform still win; children keep their own. A new job outfit is one line in `JOB_DRESS`; a new hat is a case in `hatL` (and an entry in `HATS` for the gallery).

## Seeing what villagers are doing (src/ui/hud-villager.ts)
- **Villager card**: click a villager on the canvas (`openVillagerCard`, Esc or x closes; it refreshes twice a second). It reads `n.status` (what the plan is doing now, so any step's text should say something a player can follow), mood and plain-word need hints, `Company` (the four strongest ties from `n.aff`/`n.know`/`relLabel`, in words, no numbers), lines the chronicle says about them and `n.log` (the steps before). Numbers stay in `npcDebug` (build-mode hover). New systems (gifts, courtship) should give their plan steps readable texts and write chronicle or `lfAff` reasons so the card shows them.
- **Meetings** (walking up and standing face to face before talking, resuming the interrupted plan after) were part of the old exchange code and go back in with the new engine: `docs/talk-engine.md`, "Where it runs".

## World modules (src/life2/modules.ts; docs/world-modules.md)
Switch whole jobs, outings, ceremonies, systems and talk kinds on and off to build and balance one piece at a time: `modOn('job.Farmer')`, `modOn('gath.fishing')`, `modOn('event.wedding')` (needs `sys.romance`), `sys.<LF2 feature>`, `talk.<kind>`. **J** opens the panel ("All on", "Only farming"); `MODS.only([...])` is a recipe (only what is named and what it needs); the choice is kept in `gh_mods`. Wired in `pickAgenda`, `lf2AssignJobs`, `tutFarmers`, `lfPlan` (outings), `scheduleWedding`, the funeral queue and `scheduleParty`. What is switched off lives with the island (`town.modsOff`, saved with it; `MODS.sync()` makes `LF2.feat` and `TALK.on` follow it when an island starts or loads). A new job/event/system: `MODS.define(id, label, kind, needs)` and ask `modOn(id)` where the game decides to do it.

## Ready Farming Village (src/life2/farm.ts; Islands panel, third option)
A world with only what farming needs, for balancing and testing the farm economy: `loadTown(seed, 'L', 'farm')` -> `farmStart()`: `town.scenario = 'farm'`, `town.farmEcon = true`, `prebuildVillage({ farm: true })` (houses, privies, two fields, the market square; no church, tavern, bakery, smithy, jail), the generator's own traders are made workers, and `MODS.only(...)` leaves on only `job.Farmer`, `sys.economy`, `sys.couples` and the talk kinds. Two stalls: a **seed seller** (`SEED_DEF`: sells `seed_<crop>` goods) and a **greengrocer** (`GROCER_DEF`: buys and sells grain, every crop and cut flowers), both kept by villagers (`farmSetup`). **Real money** when `town.farmEcon`: `pay()` moves coin, a shop will not buy what it cannot pay for, buyers cannot spend what they do not have (`could_not_afford`), `lf2Paid` stands down; the greengrocer's surplus above 70% of its cap is taken by the carter at 50% of base price (`town.eco.exported`) and each stall gets a small float so it never runs dry. **Seed**: farmers buy seed for the day's sowing (`farmSeedBuy`, first steps of `farmPlan`), sow from what they hold (`farmSow`; with none and the stall shut there is nothing to sow), keep one seed back from each harvest (`itHarvest`). The seed stall brings in new kinds every two days in `FARM_SEED_ORDER` (`farmSeedRange`, `farmRestock` at 6h: `new_in_stock`, noticed by farmers when they shop; `out_of_stock`, `price_up`/`price_down` noticed by the keeper); flowers (`GOODS.flowers`) are cut by farmers (harvest gives 4 flowers) and bought by households now and then (`farmFlowerBuyers`). Goods: `seed_<crop>` and `flowers` in `GOODS`; crop yields are in `FOODS` and `SELLABLE`. Talk: the life events above have first-person lines (`life:new_in_stock`, `life:out_of_stock`, `life:could_not_afford`, `life:price_up/down`, `life:bought` ...); in six days the farm world makes ~130 plantings, ~120 harvests, 18 seed purchases, ~30 produce sales and about 70 spoken lines about them. **The player** bumps a stall (`T.STALL`, control.ts) and gets a menu (`farmShopDialog`, through `lf2Dialog`): buy a seed (goes in the bag as a stack) or five, buy vegetables or flowers, sell what you carry; real prices and money. A farmer short of money (under 12) takes a crop to market at 4 items instead of 12; seed held back from harvests is capped at 12. **The bakery** (`farmSetup` builds one, `job.Baker` and `sys.cooking` are in the recipe): the existing life2 bakery (`LF2_JOBS.Baker`, `lf2Craft`, `lf2FetchPlan`) with real money here: the baker pays the greengrocer for grain, keeps what customers pay (`lf2Buy` pays the keeper), households buy bread in the morning (`lf2ShopPlan`); every counter purchase is a shop encounter (`talkService`) and `lf2Craft` reports `baked`/`cooked`/`forged`/`woven`/`sawed` by job. The counter menu for the player (`lf2TradeDialog`) speaks the keeper's lines too. Not yet: seasons, tools wearing out in this world.

## Performance tools
- `node tools/profile.mjs <seconds> <zoom> [--pan] [--cpu] [--file=other.html]`: the real frame loop, with per-stage times, frame-time percentiles and optional panning / CPU profile. Headless Chromium paints in software, so compare before/after rather than against 60 fps.
- `node tools/diff.mjs a.html b.html [--no-people]`: renders both builds at fixed views and compares pixels. Use it after any rendering change.
- Rendering rules learned: villagers are cached as sprites when zoomed out (`ART.drawDollLOD`); the lit-area texture is rebuilt at 20 Hz; the tide tile list is cached; do not skip the island overview image in `drawGround` (it shows through at close zoom).
